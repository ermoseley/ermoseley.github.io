Guiding-center dust and response-Markov SGS turbulence

Model boundary

The response-Markov model uses independent, particle-local Gaussian draws; it does not sample a spatially shared turbulent velocity field. It does update the stochastic slip state and also applies an independent spatial carrier walk. The stored Markov slip is subsequently integrated into position, producing a known finite-grain diffusion leak discussed in the Markov section. The deferred white-direct candidate removes that state but introduces a provisional response factor.

2 · Roadmap

The logic of the derivation

There are two reductions. First, we remove the deterministic fast gyrophase. Second, we represent the unresolved carrier over its SGS turnover time. The current Markov choice keeps a finite-correlation endpoint slip state and adds a memoryless carrier displacement. The deferred white-direct construction instead homogenizes the entire stochastic response into a position-only diffusivity.

The central idea

Guiding-center theory removes the phase of a fast orbit. The SGS closure should not put that phase back into the spatial trajectory. Markov therefore excites only the phase-free response variables—\(v_\parallel\) and \(\mu\)—or the physical Cartesian velocity in ORBIT, while the unresolved carrier displacement remains a separate isotropic Itô channel.

3.1 Electromagnetic units

To keep signs visible, define the electromagnetic potentials per unit grain mass,

\[ \boldsymbol{\mathcal A}\equiv\frac{q}{mc}\boldsymbol A_{\rm em}, \qquad \Phi\equiv\frac{q}{m}\phi, \qquad \nabla\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}=\Omega\bhat. \tag{N1} \]

This is the same normalization as setting \(q=m=c=1\), but it avoids hiding the fact that \(\Omega\) is signed.

3.2 Guiding-center ordering

Let \(L\) be the shortest resolved scale on which the gas or magnetic field changes. Guiding-center theory requires the orbit to be small and fast relative to that scale:

\[ \epsilon_{\rm GC}\sim \max\!\left( \frac{\rho_L}{L}, \frac{\nu}{|\Omega|}, \frac{|\nabla\boldsymbol U|}{|\Omega|}, \frac{|w_\parallel|\,|\nabla\bhat|}{|\Omega|} \right)\ll1. \tag{N2} \]
Do not confuse an algebraic limit with a valid GC limit

The perpendicular linear system below can be evaluated even when \(|\Omega|\) is small. That does not make the guiding-center expansion valid. If the Larmor radius is unresolved poorly, or if both drag and effective gyrofrequency vanish, the physical state must remain full orbit.

4 · Deterministic parent problem

Start with the full charged-particle dynamics

4.1 Configuration-space and phase-space Lagrangians

Per unit grain mass, a nonrelativistic charged particle has the Lagrangian

\[ L(\boldsymbol x,\dot{\boldsymbol x},t) =\frac12|\dot{\boldsymbol x}|^2 \boldsymbol{\mathcal A}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol x} -\Phi. \tag{P1} \]

Introduce an independent velocity coordinate \(\boldsymbol v\). The equivalent phase-space Lagrangian is

\[ \mathcal L =(\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}+\boldsymbol v)\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol x} -\frac12|\boldsymbol v|^2-\Phi. \tag{P2} \]

Varying \(\boldsymbol v\) immediately gives \(\dot{\boldsymbol x}=\boldsymbol v\). The advantage of (P2) is that its one-form can be transformed directly into guiding-center coordinates.

4.2 Drag as a Rayleigh dissipation function

Linear drag is not conservative, so it does not belong in \(\mathcal L\). Write it instead as

\[ \mathcal R =\frac{\nu}{2}\left|\dot{\boldsymbol x}-\boldsymbol U\right|^2, \qquad -\frac{\partial\mathcal R}{\partial\dot{\boldsymbol x}} =-\nu(\dot{\boldsymbol x}-\boldsymbol U). \tag{P3} \]

The dissipative Euler–Lagrange equation is

\[ \frac{\dd}{\dd t} \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot q_i} -\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial q_i} =-\frac{\partial\mathcal R}{\partial\dot q_i}. \tag{P4} \]

4.3 Recovering the Lorentz–drag equation

The \(\boldsymbol x\) variation of (P2), together with \(\dot{\boldsymbol x}=\boldsymbol v\), gives

\[ \dot{\boldsymbol v} =-\partial_t\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}-\nabla\Phi \boldsymbol v\times(\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}) -\nu(\boldsymbol v-\boldsymbol U) +\boldsymbol f_{\rm ext}. \tag{P5} \]

Under ideal MHD, \(\boldsymbol E=-\boldsymbol U\times\boldsymbol B/c\). Therefore the electric and magnetic terms combine into a force on the gas-frame slip \(\boldsymbol W\equiv\boldsymbol v-\boldsymbol U\):

\[ \boxed{ \dot{\boldsymbol v} =\boldsymbol f_{\rm ext} \Omega(\boldsymbol v-\boldsymbol U)\times\bhat -\nu(\boldsymbol v-\boldsymbol U). } \tag{P6} \]

Since \(\boldsymbol W\times\bhat=-\boldsymbol J_b\boldsymbol W\), define

\[ \boldsymbol K_r\equiv\nu\boldsymbol I+\Omega\boldsymbol J_b, \qquad \dot{\boldsymbol v} =\boldsymbol f_{\rm ext}-\boldsymbol K_r(\boldsymbol v-\boldsymbol U). \tag{P7} \]
Physical check

For uniform fields and no external force, \(\boldsymbol W\cdot[-\Omega\boldsymbol J_b\boldsymbol W]=0\): the magnetic response rotates the slip but does no work. Drag contributes \(-\nu|\boldsymbol W|^2\), so it alone removes kinetic energy.

5 · Deterministic reduction

Replace the fast orbit by guiding-center variables

The physical particle position is separated into a slowly moving center and a rapidly rotating gyroradius:

\[ \boldsymbol x=\boldsymbol X+\boldsymbol\rho, \qquad \frac{\rho_L}{L}=O(\epsilon_{\rm GC}). \tag{G1} \]

5.1 A sign-consistent gyrophase convention

Choose a right-handed basis \((\boldsymbol e_1,\boldsymbol e_2,\bhat)\) with \(\boldsymbol e_1\times\boldsymbol e_2=\bhat\). Define

\[ \rho_L=\frac{\sqrt{2J}}{|\Omega|}, \qquad \boldsymbol\rho =\rho_L(\cos\theta\,\boldsymbol e_1+\sin\theta\,\boldsymbol e_2), \tag{G2} \] \[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol v_{\rm gyr}=-\Omega\bhat\times\boldsymbol\rho, \qquad \dot\theta=-\Omega. } \tag{G3} \]

Differentiating (G2) with \(\dot\theta=-\Omega\) reproduces (G3). That simple check fixes the gyrophase sign and, later, the vorticity correction to the magnetic-moment equation.

5.2 Slow velocity and gyro-action

The gyroaveraged physical velocity is written

\[ \langle\boldsymbol v\rangle_\theta =\boldsymbol U+w_\parallel\bhat+\boldsymbol w_\perp, \qquad w_\parallel=v_\parallel-U_\parallel. \tag{G4} \]

The nonnegative moment used by the code is \(\mu=J/B\). For the variational derivation, the signed action \(I=J/\Omega\) is cleaner. Because \(\Omega/B=q/(mc)\) is constant during the operator step, \(I\) and \(\mu\) differ only by a constant signed factor.

5.3 The guiding-center Lagrangian

Define the modified one-form and its curl at fixed \(w_\parallel\):

\[ \boldsymbol{\mathcal A}^{*} =\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}+\boldsymbol U+w_\parallel\bhat, \qquad \boldsymbol B^{*} =\Omega\bhat+\boldsymbol\omega_U +w_\parallel\nabla\times\bhat, \tag{G5} \] \[ \boldsymbol\omega_U\equiv\nabla\times\boldsymbol U, \qquad \widetilde\Omega \equiv\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol B^{*} =\Omega+\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_U +w_\parallel\bhat\cdot\nabla\times\bhat. \tag{G6} \]

The Hamiltonian and phase-space Lagrangian are

\[ \mathcal H =\Phi+J+\frac12 \left|\boldsymbol U+w_\parallel\bhat\right|^2, \tag{G7} \] \[ \boxed{ \mathcal L_{\rm GC} =\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}^{*}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol X} -I\dot\theta-\mathcal H. } \tag{G8} \]

Varying \(I\) in (G8) gives \(-\dot\theta-\partial\mathcal H/\partial I=0\). Since \(J=I\Omega\), this is precisely \(\dot\theta=-\Omega\).

6 · Deterministic reduction

Gyroaverage the drag, not the equations by inspection

The GC Lagrangian handles the conservative fast orbit. Drag must be transformed separately. The goal is a slow Rayleigh function that produces drag on the center, damping of gyration energy, and the leading correction caused by inhomogeneous fields.

6.1 Expand the relative velocity

Let

\[ \boldsymbol W\equiv\dot{\boldsymbol X}-\boldsymbol U(\boldsymbol X,t), \qquad \boldsymbol U(\boldsymbol X+\boldsymbol\rho) =\boldsymbol U(\boldsymbol X) +(\boldsymbol\rho\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol U+O(\epsilon_{\rm GC}^2). \tag{R1} \]

Then

\[ \dot{\boldsymbol x}-\boldsymbol U(\boldsymbol x) =\boldsymbol W+\dot{\boldsymbol\rho} -(\boldsymbol\rho\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol U +O(\epsilon_{\rm GC}^2). \tag{R2} \]

6.2 The gyroangle averages

Write \(\boldsymbol\rho=\rho_L\boldsymbol a\) and \(\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}=\rho_L\boldsymbol c\,\dot\theta\), where \(\boldsymbol a=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\) and \(\boldsymbol c=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)\) in the perpendicular basis. The needed correlators are

\[ \langle a_i\rangle=\langle c_i\rangle=0, \quad \langle a_i a_j\rangle=\langle c_i c_j\rangle =\frac12P_{\perp,ij}, \quad \langle c_i a_j\rangle=-\frac12\epsilon_{ijk}b_k. \tag{R3} \]

The terms linear in one gyrovector vanish. The leading gyration term is

\[ \frac{\nu}{2}\langle|\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}|^2\rangle =\nu\frac{I}{\Omega}\dot\theta^2, \qquad \frac{I}{\Omega}=\frac{J}{\Omega^2}>0. \tag{R4} \]

6.3 Why gas vorticity changes the damping

The cross term between \(\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}\) and the velocity gradient survives. Using (R3),

\[ -\nu\left\langle \dot{\boldsymbol\rho}\cdot (\boldsymbol\rho\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol U \right\rangle =-\nu\frac{I}{\Omega}\dot\theta\, \bhat\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_U. \tag{R5} \]

Equations (R4) and (R5) combine into \(\nu(I/\Omega)\dot\theta(\dot\theta-\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_U)\). Co-rotation of the gas changes the relative angular velocity felt by the drag.

6.4 The first-order grad-\(B\) correction

Because \(\rho_L\propto B^{-1/2}\), the orbit scale is slightly different on opposite sides of an inhomogeneous orbit. A consistent first-order line-integral calculation gives, after using the leading on-shell phase equation,

\[ \boldsymbol v_{\nabla B} \equiv I\,\bhat\times\nabla\ln B =\frac{J}{\Omega}\bhat\times\nabla\ln B, \tag{R6} \] \[ \left\langle \boldsymbol W\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol\rho} \right\rangle_{\nabla B} =\boldsymbol W\cdot\boldsymbol v_{\nabla B}. \tag{R7} \]
A controlled asymptotic closure

Equation (R7) uses \(\dot\theta=-\Omega\) in the \(\boldsymbol X\)-sector but retains the off-shell \(\dot\theta\) dependence needed to derive moment damping. The result is a first-order GC dissipation model, not a globally exact Rayleigh functional at every order in \(\rho_L/L\).

6.5 Final guiding-center Rayleigh function

\[ \boxed{ \mathcal R_{\rm GC} =\frac{\nu}{2}|\dot{\boldsymbol X}-\boldsymbol U|^2 +\nu(\dot{\boldsymbol X}-\boldsymbol U)\cdot\boldsymbol v_{\nabla B} +\nu\frac{I}{\Omega}\dot\theta \left(\dot\theta-\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_U\right). } \tag{R8} \]
  • The first term damps the slow center motion relative to the gas.
  • The second term corrects that drag for the orbit-averaged grad-\(B\) drift.
  • The third term damps gyration energy and accounts for gas rotation about the field.

The derivation assumes a linear, phase-independent \(\nu\). A nonlinear drag law requires a separate gyrophase average. The numerical model instead freezes an effective rate from a slow, pre-noise state; Section 8 states that approximation.

7 · Deterministic result

Derive the guiding-center equations of motion

7.1 The raw \(\boldsymbol X\) equation

Apply the dissipative Euler–Lagrange equation to \(\boldsymbol X\), using (G8) and (R8). Any resolved acceleration not already contained in the ideal electromagnetic force is denoted \(\boldsymbol f\):

\[ \partial_t\boldsymbol{\mathcal A}^{*} +\nabla\mathcal H -\dot{\boldsymbol X}\times\boldsymbol B^{*} +\dot w_\parallel\bhat = -\nu(\dot{\boldsymbol X}-\boldsymbol U+\boldsymbol v_{\nabla B}) +\boldsymbol f. \tag{D1} \]

7.2 The parallel constraint

The coordinate \(w_\parallel\) has no time derivative in the Lagrangian. Its Euler–Lagrange equation is therefore a constraint:

\[ \bhat\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol X} =U_\parallel+w_\parallel=v_\parallel. \tag{D2} \]

Define the remaining perpendicular motion by

\[ \dot{\boldsymbol X} =\boldsymbol U+w_\parallel\bhat+\boldsymbol w_\perp, \qquad \bhat\cdot\boldsymbol w_\perp=0. \tag{D3} \]

7.3 Collapse the vector algebra

Introduce the derivative along the phase-free slow trajectory,

\[ \mathcal D_U \equiv\partial_t+ (\boldsymbol U+w_\parallel\bhat)\cdot\nabla, \qquad \boldsymbol G_U \equiv\mathcal D_U\boldsymbol U +w_\parallel\mathcal D_U\bhat. \tag{D4} \]

Using ideal MHD to eliminate the electric potential, (D1) reduces to

\[ \dot w_\parallel\bhat -\boldsymbol w_\perp\times\boldsymbol B^{*} = -\boldsymbol G_U -J\nabla\ln B -\nu(w_\parallel\bhat+\boldsymbol w_\perp+\boldsymbol v_{\nabla B}) +\boldsymbol f. \tag{D5} \]

7.4 Parallel motion

Project (D5) along \(\bhat\):

\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \dot w_\parallel={} & -\nu w_\parallel -\bhat\cdot\mathcal D_U\boldsymbol U -J(\bhat\cdot\nabla)\ln B \\ &+\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol w_\perp\times \left(\boldsymbol\omega_U +w_\parallel\nabla\times\bhat\right) +f_\parallel . \end{aligned}} \tag{D6} \]

The terms are, in order: parallel drag, acceleration of the gas frame, magnetic mirroring, rotation/torsion coupling, and any additional resolved parallel force.

7.5 Gyrophase and magnetic moment

The \(I\) variation already gave \(\dot\theta=-\Omega\). The \(\theta\) variation gives

\[ \dot I =\nu\frac{I}{\Omega} \left(2\dot\theta-\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_U\right) =-\nu I\left( 2+\frac{\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_U}{\Omega}\right). \tag{D7} \]

Because \(I\propto\mu\) at fixed charge-to-mass ratio,

\[ \boxed{ \dot\mu =-\nu\mu \left(2+\frac{\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_U}{\Omega}\right). } \tag{D8} \]
Sign check with rigid rotation

If \(\boldsymbol U=\gamma\,\bhat\times\boldsymbol x\), then \(\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol\omega_U=2\gamma\). A positive-\(\Omega\) gyration rotates at \(-\Omega\), so the gas-relative angular rate is \(\Omega+\gamma\). Equation (D8) gives exactly the corresponding \(2+2\gamma/\Omega\) energy-damping factor.

7.6 Perpendicular drift

Project (D5) perpendicular to \(\bhat\). Define

\[ \boldsymbol R_\perp =-\boldsymbol G_{U,\perp} -J(\nabla\ln B)_\perp -\nu\boldsymbol v_{\nabla B} +\boldsymbol f_\perp. \tag{D9} \]

The perpendicular equation is the two-dimensional linear system

\[ \nu\boldsymbol w_\perp +\widetilde\Omega\bhat\times\boldsymbol w_\perp =\boldsymbol R_\perp. \tag{D10} \]

Since \(\boldsymbol J_b^2=-\boldsymbol P_\perp\), its inverse is explicit:

\[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol w_\perp = \frac{ \nu\boldsymbol R_\perp -\widetilde\Omega\bhat\times\boldsymbol R_\perp }{\nu^2+\widetilde\Omega^2}. } \tag{D11} \]

7.7 Equivalent lab-frame parallel equation

The code stores \(v_\parallel=U_\parallel+w_\parallel\). Because the actual GC trajectory also moves with \(\boldsymbol w_\perp\), differentiating \(U_\parallel(\boldsymbol X,t)\) requires one transverse sampling term:

\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \dot v_\parallel={} & -\nu(v_\parallel-U_\parallel) -J(\bhat\cdot\nabla)\ln B +\boldsymbol U\cdot\mathcal D_U\bhat\\ &+\boldsymbol w_\perp\cdot\nabla U_\parallel +\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol w_\perp\times (\boldsymbol\omega_U+w_\parallel\nabla\times\bhat) +f_\parallel . \end{aligned}} \tag{D12} \]
Known theory–source discrepancy

The older dust_gc_v3.tex conversion omitted \(\boldsymbol w_\perp\cdot\nabla U_\parallel\). The exact continuum equation is (D12). The current frozen GC force in the Gaussian mover also omits this term, so Section 8 records that omission as a numerical closure choice rather than silently identifying it with the continuum equation.

8 · Deterministic theory-to-code map

The frozen deterministic closures used by the mover

The variational system above assumes linear drag and continuously varying fields. The current mover advances a local, coefficient-frozen approximation. This section states that approximation mathematically; programming details are reserved for the final implementation section.

8.1 The phase-free stored state

The persistent GC variables are

\[ \left(\boldsymbol X,\;v_\parallel,\;\mu\right), \qquad \mu\ge0. \tag{C1} \]

No gyrophase and no physical Larmor displacement are stored. The Cartesian velocity representative retained for advection and future gathers is

\[ \boldsymbol v_{\rm stored} =\boldsymbol U_\perp+v_\parallel\bhat+\boldsymbol w_\perp. \tag{C2} \]

It contains the slow drift but not \(\boldsymbol v_{\rm gyr}\); the gyration energy lives only in \(\mu\).

Keep position and phase state distinct

The physical particle position \(\boldsymbol x\), the guiding-center position \(\boldsymbol X\), and the stored phase-free velocity representative are different objects. The persistent GC state contains \(\boldsymbol X\), not a point on the Larmor circle. Deployed V6 nevertheless adds a phase-dependent position contribution before storing the endpoint as the next \(\boldsymbol X\). Without persistent phase, that contribution is not a coherent physical orbit segment; Section 14 quantifies the resulting finite-child walk.

8.2 Freeze the nonlinear response from a slow state

For nonlinear Epstein/Coulomb drag, a true gyroaverage would require separate phase integrals. The code instead computes a slow effective rate \(\bar\nu\) from the incoming, pre-noise state and holds it fixed during one accepted child interval. Schematically,

\[ \bar\nu =\nu_{\rm stop} \sqrt{1+C_{\rm E} \frac{w_\parallel^2+2J+|\boldsymbol w_\perp|^2}{c_s^2}} +\nu_{\rm Coul}, \tag{C3} \]

with the exact coefficient \(C_{\rm E}\) supplied by the selected drag law. A local nonlinear solve determines the slow slip used for this freeze. The white stochastic realization is never inserted into the nonlinear drag or charge law.

8.3 From the massless-dust ODE to the Padé drag operator

What “massless dust” means here

Take the test-particle limit \(\rho_d/\rho\to0\): the gas does not recoil during the drag substep. This does not set the mass of an individual grain to zero. Its finite inertia remains in the stopping rates.

The scalar drag equation

Freeze \(\boldsymbol U\), \(c_s\), and the drag coefficients. Isotropic drag preserves the direction of the gas-frame slip \(\boldsymbol w=\boldsymbol v-\boldsymbol U\), so only \(w=|\boldsymbol w|\) changes. Write

\[ \dot w=-\left[n_0\sqrt{1+\frac{w^2}{v_{\rm B}^2}}+n_1\right]w, \qquad n_0\equiv\nu_{\rm stop},\quad n_1\equiv\nu_{\rm Coul},\quad v_{\rm B}\equiv\frac{c_s}{\sqrt{C_{\rm E}}}. \tag{P1} \]

Thus \(\sqrt{1+w^2/v_{\rm B}^2}\) is exactly the Epstein–Baines transonic factor, while the frozen subsonic Coulomb rate remains additive.

The exact implicit solution

Separate variables and set

\[ \frac{\dd w}{w\left(n_0\sqrt{1+w^2/v_{\rm B}^2}+n_1\right)}=-\dd t, \qquad u\equiv\sqrt{1+\frac{w^2}{v_{\rm B}^2}}, \qquad w\,\dd w=v_{\rm B}^2u\,\dd u, \tag{P2} \] \[ \int\frac{u\,\dd u} {(u-1)(u+1)(n_0u+n_1)}=-t+\mathrm{constant}. \tag{P3} \]

Let \(R(w)=\sqrt{v_{\rm B}^2+w^2}\), with \(R_0=R(w_0)\). For \(n_0\ne n_1\), the partial-fraction integral gives the exact time required to decay from \(w_0\) to \(w\):

\[ \boxed{ t=\frac{1}{n_1^2-n_0^2} \left[ (n_1-n_0)\ln\frac{w_0}{w} +n_0\ln\frac{v_{\rm B}+R_0}{v_{\rm B}+R} +n_1\ln\frac{n_1v_{\rm B}+n_0R} {n_1v_{\rm B}+n_0R_0} \right].} \tag{P4} \]

Equation (P4) is exact but transcendental in \(w\). Pure Epstein–Baines drag, \(n_1=0\), can be inverted explicitly:

\[ u(t)= \frac{\sinh(n_0t)+u_0\cosh(n_0t)} {\cosh(n_0t)+u_0\sinh(n_0t)}, \qquad w(t)=v_{\rm B}\sqrt{u(t)^2-1}. \tag{P5} \]
The degenerate case \(n_0=n_1\)

For \(n_0=n_1\equiv n\), the repeated pole gives

\[ \ln\!\frac{w^2}{(v_{\rm B}+R)^2} -\frac{2v_{\rm B}}{v_{\rm B}+R} = \ln\!\frac{w_0^2}{(v_{\rm B}+R_0)^2} -\frac{2v_{\rm B}}{v_{\rm B}+R_0} -4nt. \tag{P6} \]

Expand the reciprocal, not the speed

The inexpensive update follows without inverting (P4). Define \(y=1/w\), \(g=n_0u+n_1\), and differentiate along the exact ODE:

\[ \dot y=gy, \qquad \dot u=-g\frac{u^2-1}{u}, \qquad \dot g=-g n_0\left(u-\frac1u\right). \tag{P7} \] \[ \ddot y=(\dot g+g^2)y =g\left(\frac{n_0}{u}+n_1\right)y \equiv gh\,y, \qquad h\equiv\frac{n_0}{u}+n_1. \tag{P8} \]

At the beginning of a step, therefore,

\[ \frac{1}{w(t+\Delta t)} =\frac{1}{w_n} \left[1+g_n\Delta t+\tfrac12g_nh_n\Delta t^2\right] +O(\Delta t^3), \tag{P9} \] \[ g_n=n_0u_n+n_1, \qquad h_n=\frac{n_0}{u_n}+n_1, \qquad u_n=\sqrt{1+\frac{w_n^2}{v_{\rm B}^2}}. \tag{P10} \]

Taking the reciprocal of the truncated series gives the implemented map:

\[ \boxed{ w_{n+1} =\frac{w_n} {1+g_n\Delta t+\tfrac12g_nh_n\Delta t^2}, \qquad \boldsymbol w_{n+1}=\frac{w_{n+1}}{w_n}\boldsymbol w_n.} \tag{P11} \]

This is explicit in the old state—despite the historical helper name fully_implicit_drag—and requires no nonlinear solve. In the zero-slip case, \(w_n=0\) is a fixed point and the code returns zero directly; the vector ratio in (P11) is used only for \(w_n>0\). In the linear limit \(u_n\to1\), let \(z=(n_0+n_1)\Delta t\). Then

\[ \frac{w_{n+1}}{w_n} =R_{02}(z) \equiv\frac{1}{1+z+z^2/2}, \qquad R_{02}(z)=e^{-z}+O(z^3), \qquad \lim_{z\to+\infty}R_{02}(z)=0. \tag{P12} \]

Thus the nonlinear map is second order, positive, monotone, and inherits the L-stable decay of the \([0/2]\) Padé approximant. For pure Epstein–Baines drag, \(g_nh_n=n_0^2\): the transonic factors cancel in the quadratic coefficient.

Rotation does not alter the decay

For a full orbit, add gyromotion to the vector equation:

\[ \dot{\boldsymbol w} =-\nu(w)\boldsymbol w-\Omega\bhat\times\boldsymbol w. \tag{P13} \] \[ \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\frac{w^2}{2} =-\nu(w)w^2 -\Omega\underbrace{\boldsymbol w\cdot (\bhat\times\boldsymbol w)}_{0}. \tag{P14} \]

Hence \(\dot w=-\nu(w)w\) exactly. Radial Padé decay and the norm-preserving Lorentz rotation act on independent pieces of \(\boldsymbol w=w\widehat{\boldsymbol e}\); the drag–rotation–drag composition introduces no magnitude commutator error.

Domain and operator ownership

The \(n_1\) derivation assumes a drift-independent Coulomb rate over the step, as in the implemented frozen subsonic closure. A supersonic velocity-dependent Coulomb rolloff changes (P8) and therefore the second-order coefficient. The standalone Padé map belongs to the resolved Cartesian drag–Lorentz path. When the augmented Gaussian–Itô map owns the same frozen drag response, this standalone operator is bypassed to avoid double counting.

8.4 Affine perpendicular slip

Define the field-line curvature and perpendicular shear

\[ \boldsymbol\kappa_b=(\bhat\cdot\nabla)\bhat, \qquad \boldsymbol S_\perp =\boldsymbol P_\perp[(\bhat\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol U]. \tag{C4} \]

The MHD momentum equation supplies a local model for \(D_t\boldsymbol U\); in the code’s magnetic units,

\[ (D_t\boldsymbol U)_{\rm model} = \frac{-\nabla p-B\nabla B+B^2\boldsymbol\kappa_b}{\rho} +\boldsymbol f_{{\rm gas},{\rm resolved}}. \tag{C5} \]

The \(J\)-independent base term is the perpendicular acceleration of the grain relative to this accelerating gas frame:

\[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol F_{\rm base} \equiv \boldsymbol P_\perp \left[ \boldsymbol f_{{\rm grain},{\rm resolved}} -(D_t\boldsymbol U)_{\rm model} \right]. } \tag{C5a} \]

Here the two \(\boldsymbol f\)'s contain resolved, non-Lorentz accelerations applied directly to the indicated component. A common body force, such as gravity acting equally per unit mass on gas and dust, therefore cancels. An acceleration applied only to the gas does not cancel and enters \(\boldsymbol F_{\rm base}\) with a minus sign.

Current driven-box boundary

The implemented turbulent driver adds \(\boldsymbol a_{\rm turb}=\boldsymbol f_{\rm turb}\) to the gas momentum only. Consequently the consistent GC slip contains \(-\boldsymbol P_\perp\boldsymbol a_{\rm turb}\). The present mover constructs \((D_t\boldsymbol U)_{\rm model}\) from the MHD force and, when enabled, gravity, but does not yet gather \(\boldsymbol f_{\rm turb}\). Thus its current \(\boldsymbol F_{\rm base}\) omits this resolved forcing response.

With \(\bar\nu\) frozen, the slow perpendicular force is affine in \(J=\mu B\):

\[ \boldsymbol R_\perp(J)=\boldsymbol R_0+J\boldsymbol R_1, \tag{C6} \] \[ \boldsymbol R_0 =\boldsymbol F_{\rm base} -w_\parallel^2\boldsymbol\kappa_b -2w_\parallel\boldsymbol S_\perp, \qquad \boldsymbol R_1 =-\frac{ \nabla_\perp B+ (\bar\nu/\Omega)\bhat\times\nabla B }{B}. \tag{C7} \]

Therefore

\[ \boldsymbol w_\perp(J) =(\bar\nu\boldsymbol I+\widetilde\Omega\boldsymbol J_b)^{-1}_{\perp} [\boldsymbol R_0+J\boldsymbol R_1] \equiv\boldsymbol w_0+J\boldsymbol c_J. \tag{C8} \]

This affine form is central: it lets the full-orbit-to-GC reduction solve for \(J\) analytically and lets the code certify all possible phases before drawing one.

8.5 The slow GC force passed to the physical response map

The later Gaussian map already owns the uniform drag–gyro response. It must therefore receive only the remaining slow GC force. Define

\[ \begin{aligned} f_{\parallel,\rm GC}^{\rm code}={} & \boldsymbol U_\perp\cdot(\bhat\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol U +w_\parallel\boldsymbol U_\perp\cdot\boldsymbol\kappa_b -J(\bhat\cdot\nabla)\ln B\\ &+f_{{\rm resolved},\parallel} +\bhat\cdot\boldsymbol w_\perp\times (\boldsymbol\omega_U+w_\parallel\nabla\times\bhat). \end{aligned} \tag{C9} \]

The frozen physical-space force used for a GC trial is then

\[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol f_{\rm GC} =\boldsymbol R_0+J\boldsymbol R_1 +(\Omega-\widetilde\Omega)\bhat\times\boldsymbol w_\perp +f_{\parallel,\rm GC}^{\rm code}\bhat. } \tag{C10} \]
What is exact here—and what is frozen

For the selected local \(\bar\nu,\Omega,\bhat\), the algebraic inversion (C8) and the later linear drag–gyro propagator are exact. The gather, nonlinear rate, gradients, and slow force are nevertheless frozen over the child. Equation (C9) is the current source closure and does not contain the continuum transverse term \(\boldsymbol w_\perp\cdot\nabla U_\parallel\). In a driven box, the source also omits \(-\boldsymbol a_{{\rm turb},\perp}\) because fturb is absent from the gathered gas material acceleration; the turbulent-driving subsection of the Markov model isolates that separate plumbing gap.

Core stochastic model · Current simulation choice

The response-Markov model used by the fast mover

The source calls this option markov_legacy. It is a compact surrogate for a grain responding to one unresolved turbulent eddy: it retains a stochastic relative-velocity state from one child to the next, and it separately gives the particle the ordinary SGS carrier displacement. “Markov” means that the next endpoint depends only on the present particle state and the coefficients frozen for the current child, not on a stored history of the turbulent field.

The model in one line

Advance the deterministic GC or ORBIT response; add an exact-discrete Gaussian endpoint innovation with the desired stationary slip variance; then add an independent isotropic spatial Itô step with diffusivity \(\kappa_0\).

M.1 Carrier scales supplied by the SGS model

Let \(e_{\rm SGS}\) be the unresolved turbulent energy density, \(\rho_g\) the gas density, and \(\Delta\) the width of the gathered cell. The code converts these to a one-dimensional turbulent speed, an eddy time, a tracer diffusivity, and an acceleration-power scale:

\[ \boxed{ \sigma^2=\frac{2e_{\rm SGS}}{3\rho_g},\qquad \tau_c=\frac{\Delta}{\sigma},\qquad \kappa_0=\Delta\sigma,\qquad D_{\rm turb}=\frac{\sigma^3}{\Delta}=\frac{\sigma^2}{\tau_c}.} \tag{RM1} \]

Here \(\sigma^2\) is the variance of one unresolved carrier-velocity component. The response-Markov endpoint variance is proportional to \(\sigma^2\), not to \(D_{\rm turb}\). This matters: \(D_{\rm turb}\) has the dimensions of velocity-squared per time and is not itself a velocity variance.

M.2 Finite-correlation drag and gyro response

Freeze the effective drag and gyro rates during one child and compare them with the SGS turnover time:

\[ x=\nu_{\rm eff}\tau_c,\qquad y=\Omega_{\rm eff}\tau_c. \tag{RM2} \]

Solving the linear response to an exponentially correlated carrier gives the stationary, per-component relative-velocity variances

\[ S_\parallel=\frac{1}{1+x},\qquad S_\perp=\frac{1+x}{(1+x)^2+y^2}, \tag{RM3} \] \[ \boxed{ V_\parallel\equiv\langle w_\parallel^2\rangle =\sigma^2S_\parallel,\qquad V_\perp\equiv\langle w_{\perp,i}^2\rangle =\sigma^2S_\perp.} \tag{RM4} \]
Weak drag

\(x\ll1\)

The grain fails to follow the unresolved gas velocity, so its relative speed can approach the carrier speed.

Strong drag

\(x\gg1\)

The grain follows the carrier more closely and both relative-velocity variances decrease.

Strong gyro response

\(|y|\gg1+x\)

The perpendicular relative-velocity variance is further reduced as \(S_\perp\propto y^{-2}\).

M.3 Exact-discrete endpoint update

The deterministic mover already supplies the drag attenuation over a numerical child of duration \(h\). If \(A\) is that attenuation, the Markov innovation is chosen so that the requested stationary variance is independent of \(h\):

\[ A=e^{-\nu_{\rm eff}h},\qquad Q_\parallel=V_\parallel(1-A^2),\qquad Q_\perp=V_\perp(1-A^2). \tag{RM5} \] \[ \delta\boldsymbol w =\sqrt{Q_\parallel}(\bhat\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol\xi_v)\bhat +\sqrt{Q_\perp}\left[ \boldsymbol\xi_v-(\bhat\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol\xi_v)\bhat\right], \qquad \boldsymbol\xi_v\sim\mathcal N(\boldsymbol0,\boldsymbol I_3). \tag{RM6} \]

Equation (RM6) is the Cartesian ORBIT form. In GC coordinates, the same draw is represented by a Gaussian innovation in \(v_\parallel\) and a two-normal, noncentral-chi-square update of the magnetic moment,

\[ \mu_{n+1} =\left(\sqrt{\mu_{\rm det}}+\sqrt{c_\mu}\,\xi_1\right)^2 +c_\mu\xi_2^2, \tag{RM7} \]

where the code chooses \(c_\mu\) from \(V_\perp\), \(B\), and the same Padé attenuation used by the deterministic moment update. This keeps \(\mu\ge0\) and gives the two perpendicular degrees of freedom the intended stationary energy. Afterward, the algebraic perpendicular slip is recomputed and the realized state is checked against the GC validity conditions.

M.4 Independent spatial carrier walk

The endpoint innovation does not replace turbulent advection by the unresolved gas. The current model therefore also applies

\[ \boxed{ \Delta\boldsymbol X_{\rm car} =h\nabla\kappa_0+\sqrt{2\kappa_0h}\,\boldsymbol\xi_x, \qquad \boldsymbol\xi_x\sim\mathcal N(\boldsymbol0,\boldsymbol I_3),} \tag{RM8} \]

with \(\boldsymbol\xi_x\) independent of \(\boldsymbol\xi_v\). This is the Itô representation of volume-Fickian diffusion, \(\partial_t\rho_d=\nabla\!\cdot(\kappa_0\nabla\rho_d)\). The current Markov path uses the full isotropic \(\kappa_0\), with no grain-response factor and no \(\kappa_0\nabla\ln\rho_g\) concentration-mixing drift. The GC and ORBIT branches receive the same spatial law.

One accepted child in the implementation

The mover gathers one frozen coefficient set, executes the deterministic GC/ORBIT map, and uses six keyed float32 Gaussian normals: three for the velocity-or-moment endpoint and three for position. In GC coordinates it then reconstructs the slow perpendicular slip; in ORBIT coordinates it retains the Cartesian relative velocity. The position draw remains an independent channel.

M.5 Known long-time diffusion leak

The physical colored-response slip has no zero-frequency power: in the parallel channel, \(w(\omega)=-i\omega u'(\omega)/(\nu+i\omega)\), so it should add no long-time diffusion beyond the carrier walk. The Markov surrogate is different: its stored OU-like velocity is integrated by later deterministic children. For a scalar channel this adds

\[ D_{\rm leak} =Vh\frac{1+A}{2(1-A)} \longrightarrow\frac{V}{\nu_{\rm eff}}, \tag{RM9} \] \[ \frac{D_{{\rm leak},\parallel}}{\kappa_0} \longrightarrow\frac{1}{x(1+x)}. \tag{RM10} \]
Real, but not the dominant overdiffusion previously suspected

A dedicated diagnostic at one production checkpoint found family-mean parallel leaks of about 0.94–14.7% of \(\kappa_0\). The correction is therefore finite and model-dependent, but those measurements do not support a many-fold excess. The clean eventual repair is a correlated carrier/slip process whose relative-slip autocovariance integrates to zero; simply subtracting \(D_{\rm leak}\) from \(\kappa_0\) can make the remaining diffusivity negative.

M.6 Did this include the turbulent driving acceleration?

No—not as an explicit force in the guiding-center equations

The Markov stochastic closure does use SGS turbulence: the gathered \(e_{\rm SGS}\) sets \(\sigma\), \(\tau_c\), \(\kappa_0\), and both stochastic draws. But the separate resolved driving array fturb is not gathered into the particle kernel and is absent from the modeled gas material acceleration used by the deterministic GC closure.

The code currently constructs

\[ (\mathcal D_U\boldsymbol U)_{\rm code} =\frac{-\nabla p-\nabla(B^2/2)+(\boldsymbol B\!\cdot\!\nabla)\boldsymbol B} {\rho_g}+\boldsymbol g, \tag{RM11} \] \[ \boldsymbol F_{\rm base} =\boldsymbol f_{{\rm dust},\perp} -(\mathcal D_U\boldsymbol U)_{{\rm code},\perp}. \tag{RM12} \]

In a driven box, the gas equation instead contains \((\mathcal D_U\boldsymbol U)_{\rm true} =(\mathcal D_U\boldsymbol U)_{\rm code}+\boldsymbol a_{\rm turb}\). The driver accelerates the gas, not the dust, so the relative-velocity equation requires the additional term \(-\boldsymbol a_{{\rm turb},\perp}\) in \(\boldsymbol F_{\rm base}\). It is presently missing.

9 · Deferred white-direct target

A concentration-mixing target for the position-only alternative

We now leave the deterministic derivation complete and introduce unresolved turbulence. This section develops the concentration-Fickian target proposed for the deferred white-direct alternative. It is not the law implemented by the current Markov path, which uses the volume-Fickian carrier walk in §M.4.

9.1 Candidate target: mix dust concentration

Let \(\rho_d\) and \(\rho_g\) be the dust and gas mass densities, and let \(\boldsymbol v_{\rm det}\) be the velocity supplied by the deterministic GC or ORBIT mover. The selected closure mixes the concentration \(\rho_d/\rho_g\):

\[ \boxed{ \partial_t\rho_d+ \nabla\cdot(\rho_d\boldsymbol v_{\rm det}) =\nabla\cdot\left[ \rho_g\kappa_d\nabla\left(\frac{\rho_d}{\rho_g}\right) \right]. } \tag{S1} \]

An isotropic Itô particle process with this forward equation is

\[ \dd\boldsymbol X =\left(\boldsymbol v_{\rm det}+\boldsymbol a_{\rm mix}\right)\dd t +\sqrt{2\kappa_d}\,\dd\boldsymbol W, \tag{S2} \] \[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol a_{\rm mix} =\nabla\kappa_d+\kappa_d\nabla\ln\rho_g.} \tag{S3} \]

Its Fokker–Planck diffusion term is

\[ -\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol a_{\rm mix}\rho_d) +\nabla^2(\kappa_d\rho_d) =\nabla\cdot\left[ \rho_g\kappa_d\nabla\left(\frac{\rho_d}{\rho_g}\right) \right]. \tag{S4} \]

The \(\nabla\kappa_d\) term removes the spurious accumulation caused by multiplicative noise; \(\kappa_d\nabla\ln\rho_g\) makes uniform dust-to-gas ratio, rather than uniform dust volume density, the static equilibrium.

9.2 The carrier coefficient and its physical clock

If \(e_{\rm SGS}\) is the unresolved kinetic-energy density, the unresolved one-dimensional velocity dispersion and carrier diffusivity are

\[ \sigma_{\rm 1D}^2=\frac{2e_{\rm SGS}}{3\rho_g}, \qquad \boxed{\kappa_0=\Delta\sigma_{\rm 1D}}, \tag{S5} \] \[ \boxed{ \tau_\Delta=\frac{\Delta^2}{\kappa_0} =\frac{\Delta}{\sigma_{\rm 1D}}.} \tag{S6} \]
What the mesh supplies

The gathered kappa_sgs is \(\kappa_0\), already normalized with the one-dimensional dispersion. It must not be divided by \(\sqrt3\) again. The numerical child duration \(h\) does not define the eddy. The response coefficient below depends on \(\tau_\Delta\), so changing dust subcycling does not change the physical diffusivity.

9.3 The drag-channel white-turnover response

To determine how much of the carrier displacement reaches the grain, consider a first-order linear response over one eddy lifetime. With \(\nu_{\rm lin}\) the linear stopping rate, the displacement kernel is \(1-e^{-\nu_{\rm lin}(\tau_\Delta-s)}\). Its mean-square gain is

\[ z\equiv\nu_{\rm lin}\tau_\Delta, \qquad f(z)\equiv\frac{1}{\tau_\Delta} \int_0^{\tau_\Delta} \left[1-e^{-\nu_{\rm lin}(\tau_\Delta-s)}\right]^2\dd s, \tag{S7} \]
\[ \boxed{ f(z)=1-\frac{3}{2z}+\frac{2e^{-z}}{z} -\frac{e^{-2z}}{2z}.} \tag{S8} \]

Equation (S8) is the neutral, parallel, drag-only response. The candidate does not store or kick a subgrid velocity. Its response rate is \(\nu_{\rm lin}=\nu_{\rm stop}+\nu_{\rm Coul}\). The nonlinear Epstein–Baines correction remains in the deterministic drag map.

9.4 The v2 magnetized-trace candidate

The current working tree introduces the static magnetization fraction

\[ m=\frac{\Omega^2}{\nu_{\rm lin}^2+\Omega^2}, \qquad f_{\parallel}=f, \qquad f_{\perp,{\rm v2}}=1-(1-f)(1-m), \tag{S9} \] \[ \boxed{ F(z,m)=\frac{f_\parallel+2f_{\perp,{\rm v2}}}{3} =f(z)+\frac23[1-f(z)]m, \qquad \kappa_d=\kappa_0F.} \tag{S10} \]

This preserves one isotropic three-normal walk while matching the trace of the assumed tensor. For \(m\to0\), v2 returns the neutral response \(F=f\). For \(m\to1\), it assigns unit gain to two perpendicular channels and \(F\to(f+2)/3\). The motivation is the ideal-MHD \(\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B\) drift of a magnetized guiding center.

The \(2/3\) floor is a closure assumption, not an all-frequency result

The usual \(\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B\) drift is the adiabatic guiding-center response; it requires the forcing to be slow compared with gyromotion. Equation (S9) depends only on \(|\Omega|/\nu_{\rm lin}\) and omits the independent turnover parameter \(y=|\Omega|\tau_\Delta\). Scaling both rates to zero at fixed ratio leaves \(m\), and therefore the v2 floor, finite even though the grain cannot respond within one eddy lifetime.

A response derived consistently from the same white-window kernel would instead retain both \(z\) and \(y\):

\[ g_\perp(z,y) =1+\varphi_1(2z)-2\operatorname{Re}\varphi_1(z+iy), \qquad F_{\rm win}=\frac{f(z)+2g_\perp(z,y)}{3}. \tag{S11} \]

This is the position scalar already derived in (M22), evaluated on \(\tau_\Delta\). It gives \(g_\perp\simeq(z^2+y^2)/3\) when both rates are slow. Whether (S11) or a colored-eddy alternative is the desired physics must be decided before v2's magnetized trace is called final. The decisive population diagnostic must therefore include \(y\), not only \(z\), \(f\), and \(m\).

As an analytic scale check, the values quoted in Claude’s explainer, \(\kappa_0=1.328\times10^{-3}\), \(\Delta=1.94\times10^{-3}\), and \(|\Omega|\simeq310\), give \(\tau_\Delta\simeq2.83\times10^{-3}\) and \(y\simeq0.88\). For \(\nu_{\rm lin}=1.5\)–\(6.1\), v2 gives \(F\simeq0.667\), whereas (S11) gives \(F_{\rm win}\simeq0.16\): about a factor of four less diffusion. This is an inference from quoted coefficients, not a device or production-run measurement.

9.5 Spatial gradients of the v2 diffusivity

Let \(w=1-2m/3\). Treating \(\Delta\) as locally constant inside the gather stencil, the code evaluates

9.6 The density-gradient regularization in the mover

The continuum target (S1) requires the full \(\nabla\ln\rho_g\). V2 instead rescales this vector when its magnitude exceeds one inverse cell:

\[ \rho_f=\max(\rho_g,{\tt smallr}), \qquad \boldsymbol G_\rho =\frac{\nabla\rho_g/\rho_f} {\max\!\left(1,\Delta|\nabla\rho_g|/\rho_f\right)}, \qquad \boldsymbol a_{{\rm mix},{\rm code}} =\nabla\kappa_d+\kappa_d\boldsymbol G_\rho. \tag{S16} \]
The limiter changes the stated continuum equilibrium when it fires

It prevents a finite but arbitrarily large drift near the density floor and makes the mover agree with the lean scheduler's assumed bound. It also means that a uniform \(\rho_d/\rho_g\) is no longer an exact stationary solution in limited cells. The physically cleaner minimal alternative is to retain the gathered drift and include its exact magnitude in the deterministic pre-draw child cap. If density regularization is still required, it should be defined as an explicit grid-scale filtered density in both the target equation and code.

What is actually host-validated

The new host suite checks \(f\), \(zf'\), and \(f'\), both numerical branch seams, \(F(z,m)\), the full chain rule (S12) against finite differences, fail-closed arithmetic, and the unlimited concentration-Fickian identity. It passed 41/41 cases on Stellar with gfortran 8.5. It does not establish the physical form of (S9), validate the active density limiter in the Fokker–Planck equilibrium, or compile the CUDA mover.

\[ \boldsymbol K_r=\nu\boldsymbol I+\Omega\boldsymbol J_b, \qquad \boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\equiv\sqrt2\,\boldsymbol L_\star. \tag{F1} \]

11 · Retired joint-Gaussian reference

Integrate the historical phase-space model exactly

Over a child interval of duration \(h\), hold \(\boldsymbol U,\boldsymbol K_r,\boldsymbol K_\star, \boldsymbol a_{\rm drive}\), and the slow force fixed. The local SDE is then linear, so both its mean and its joint position–velocity covariance are analytic.

11.1 Deterministic kernels

Define the response and its first two time integrals:

\[ \boldsymbol E_h=e^{-\boldsymbol K_rh}, \qquad \boldsymbol M_1(h)=\int_0^h e^{-\boldsymbol K_rs}\,\dd s, \qquad \boldsymbol M_2(h)=\int_0^h(h-s)e^{-\boldsymbol K_rs}\,\dd s. \tag{M1} \]

These integral definitions remain valid when \(\boldsymbol K_r\) is singular. Let

\[ \boldsymbol W_0 =\boldsymbol V_0-\boldsymbol U-\boldsymbol a_{\rm drive}. \tag{M2} \]

The exact frozen mean is

\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \boldsymbol V_1={} & \boldsymbol U+\boldsymbol a_{\rm drive} +\boldsymbol E_h\boldsymbol W_0 +\boldsymbol M_1(h)\boldsymbol f_{\rm slow} +\boldsymbol\eta_v,\\ \boldsymbol X_1={} & \boldsymbol X_0+h(\boldsymbol U+\boldsymbol a_{\rm drive}) +\boldsymbol M_1(h)\boldsymbol W_0 +\boldsymbol M_2(h)\boldsymbol f_{\rm slow} +\boldsymbol\eta_x. \end{aligned}} \tag{M3} \]

A full-orbit step uses \(\boldsymbol f_{\rm slow}=\boldsymbol f_{\rm orb}\); a GC trial uses \(\boldsymbol f_{\rm slow}=\boldsymbol f_{\rm GC}\) from (C10). In the current source, \(\boldsymbol f_{\rm orb}\) is gravity when gravity is compiled, and otherwise zero.

11.2 The exact joint physical Gaussian block

Solving (F2) gives the correlated endpoint noises

\[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol\eta_v =\int_0^h e^{-\boldsymbol K_r(h-s)} \boldsymbol K_r\boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\,\dd\boldsymbol W_s, } \tag{M4} \] \[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol\eta_x =\int_0^h [\boldsymbol I-e^{-\boldsymbol K_r(h-s)}] \boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\,\dd\boldsymbol W_s. } \tag{M5} \]

They are driven by the same Wiener path. Consequently, \((\boldsymbol\eta_x,\boldsymbol\eta_v)\) is one six-dimensional Gaussian—not two independent three-dimensional kicks:

\[ \begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol\eta_x\\\boldsymbol\eta_v\end{bmatrix} =\boldsymbol C_h\boldsymbol\zeta, \qquad \boldsymbol C_h\boldsymbol C_h^{\mathsf T}=\boldsymbol Q_h, \qquad \boldsymbol\zeta\sim\mathcal N(\boldsymbol0,\boldsymbol I_6). \tag{M6} \]
What retired V6 drew

Equations (M4)–(M6) are the exact joint law of the frozen child, and they remain the reference against which any phase-space reduction is judged. Retired V6 deliberately draws the uncorrelated reduction instead: independent \(\boldsymbol\eta_x\) and \(\boldsymbol\eta_v\) carrying the marginal variances of \(\boldsymbol Q_{xx}\) and \(\boldsymbol Q_{vv}\), with \(\boldsymbol Q_{xv}\equiv\boldsymbol0\).

Setting \(\boldsymbol Q_{xv}=\boldsymbol0\) is a finite-child storage and model reduction; it does not enforce the volume-Fickian law at finite \(h\). It preserves the one-child marginals \(\boldsymbol Q_{xx}\) and \(\boldsymbol Q_{vv}\), but removes the causal correlation produced when the same unresolved history first changes velocity and then displacement. The position marginal becomes Fickian only in the uniformly contracting limit. Under child subdivision, the omitted cross block changes the later velocity–position coupling and can produce either under-diffusion or over-diffusion, depending on \((\nu h,\Omega h)\). Restoring this cross block would not by itself repair the separate fresh-phase GC position closure.

Exact field-aligned covariance of the reference response

For isotropic \(\boldsymbol K_\star=\kappa_d\boldsymbol I\), define

Use the entire functions

\[ \varphi_k(z) =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-z)^n}{(n+k)!}, \quad \varphi_1(z)=\frac{1-e^{-z}}{z}, \quad \varphi_2(z)=\frac{1-\varphi_1(z)}{z}, \quad \varphi_3(z)=\frac{\tfrac12-\varphi_2(z)}{z}. \tag{M13} \]

Write the tensor blocks as

\[ \begin{aligned} \boldsymbol Q_{xx}&=q_1\boldsymbol P_\parallel+q_4\boldsymbol P_\perp,\\ \boldsymbol Q_{xv}&=q_2\boldsymbol P_\parallel +q_5\boldsymbol P_\perp+q_6\boldsymbol J_b,\\ \boldsymbol Q_{vv}&=q_3\boldsymbol P_\parallel+q_7\boldsymbol P_\perp. \end{aligned} \tag{M14} \]

\[ q_1=2\kappa_d h \left[w^2\varphi_3(w)-2x^2\varphi_3(x)\right], \tag{M15} \] \[ q_2=2\kappa_d h^2\nu^2 \left[2\varphi_2(w)-\varphi_2(x)\right], \qquad q_3=2\kappa_d h\nu^2\varphi_1(w), \tag{M16} \] \[ q_4=2\kappa_d h \left[w^2\varphi_3(w) -2\,\mathrm{Re}\{u^2\varphi_3(u)\}\right], \tag{M17} \] \[ q_5+iq_6 =2\kappa_d h^2\bar\lambda \left[ 2\nu\varphi_2(w) -\bar\lambda\,\overline{\varphi_2(u)} \right], \tag{M18} \] \[ q_7=2\kappa_d h(\nu^2+\Omega^2)\varphi_1(w). \tag{M19} \]

Equations (M15)–(M19) are compact but contain severe cancellation in weak and stiff limits. The exact correlated law retains all seven scalars. It does not construct the seven-scalar factor. The compact equations define the analytic reference; they should not be copied as a naive floating-point algorithm.

11.4 Cancellation-free forms of the scalars

The chain \(\varphi_3=(\tfrac12-\varphi_2)/z\), \(\varphi_2=(1-\varphi_1)/z\) telescopes exactly:

\[ \boxed{\,z^2\varphi_3(z)=\frac z2-1+\varphi_1(z)\,} \tag{M20} \]

Substituting with \(w=2x\) cancels the \(O(x)\) terms analytically rather than numerically, and removes \(\varphi_2\) and \(\varphi_3\) from the scalars entirely:

\[ q_1=2\kappa_d h\left[1+\varphi_1(w)-2\varphi_1(x)\right], \tag{M21} \] \[ q_4=2\kappa_d h\left[1+\varphi_1(w) -2\,\operatorname{Re}\varphi_1(u)\right], \tag{M22} \] \[ q_2=\kappa_d\left(1-e^{-x}\right)^2, \tag{M23} \] \[ q_5+iq_6=2\kappa_d\,\bar u \left[\overline{\varphi_1(u)}-\varphi_1(w)\right]. \tag{M24} \]

Every bracket is \(O(1)\) built from \(O(1)\) quantities, since \(|\varphi_1|\le1\) for \(\operatorname{Re}z\ge0\) and \(\varphi_1\to0\) as \(|z|\to\infty\). The cancellation that (M15)–(M19) suffer scales with the drag stiffness \(x=\nu h\), not with the gyro stiffness \(\Omega h\): because \(\operatorname{Re}\{u^2\varphi_3(u)\} =x/2-1+\operatorname{Re}\varphi_1(u)\), the terms that cancel are \(O(x)\).

The volume-Fickian position variance is recovered in the uniformly contracting limit \(x=\nu h\to\infty\) with \(\Omega/\nu\) controlled. In that limit, \(q_1\to2\kappa_d h\) and \(q_4\to2\kappa_d h\), the Fickian position variance for diffusivity \(\kappa_d\) over an interval \(h\). Large \(|\Omega h|\) alone is not this limit: pure gyromotion does not contract the fast velocity. Indeed, as \(x\to0\), (M24) gives \(q_5=2\kappa_d(1-\cos y)\) and \(q_6=2\kappa_d(y-\sin y)\).

The weak-argument branch still needs a series, because there the cancellation is intrinsic: the bracket is itself \(O(x^2)\). Expanding it directly is cancellation-free,

\[ 1+\varphi_1(2x)-2\varphi_1(x) =\sum_{n\ge2}(-x)^n\,\frac{2^n-2}{(n+1)!}, \tag{M25} \]

and the same construction applies to (M22) with \((-2x)^n-2\operatorname{Re}(-u)^n\) in the numerator.

What the cross covariance buys, and what dropping it costs

The same unresolved history first changes velocity and then contributes to displacement, and \(\boldsymbol Q_{xv}\) stores that causal correlation. What it buys is exact composition in law (§15.1): a child of \(h_1\) followed by \(h_2\) has the same law as one child of \(h_1+h_2\). That property matters here because the tier ladder subdivides differently per grain, so exact composition is what makes tier assignment statistically neutral.

Dropping it (§11.2) does not preserve that neutrality at finite response. The ballistic scaling \(q_1\to\tfrac23\kappa_d\nu^2h^3\) is one visible failure mode, but not the whole error: whenever the endpoint velocity remains dynamically relevant, it contributes to later displacement, and the missing correlation changes the repeated-child long-time diffusivity. Section 15 gives the resulting parallel and perpendicular diffusion coefficients explicitly. A mismatch between subcycled and non-subcycled grains is therefore a direct diagnostic of the deployed reduction, not merely a remote short-time corner.

12 · Reference limiting cases

Limits that reveal what the retired response model meant

12.1 Exactly decoupled grain

\[ \nu=0,\quad\Omega=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boldsymbol K_r=\boldsymbol0,\quad \boldsymbol Q_h=\boldsymbol0,\quad \boldsymbol\eta_x=\boldsymbol\eta_v=\boldsymbol0. \tag{L1} \]

A grain that responds neither through drag nor through the Lorentz force does not receive SGS transport. The code also sets \(\boldsymbol a_{\rm drive}=\boldsymbol0\) on this branch so SGS-on and SGS-off trajectories are bitwise identical.

12.2 Pure drag

When \(\Omega=0\), the response is isotropic: \(\boldsymbol E_h=e^{-\nu h}\boldsymbol I\). Parallel and perpendicular covariance scalars coincide. This is the familiar inertial-particle Ornstein–Uhlenbeck response.

12.3 Pure gyromotion is not decoupling

For \(\nu=0\), \(\Omega\ne0\), and \(y=\Omega h\), the response has no parallel channel but retains a finite perpendicular channel:

\[ \boldsymbol Q_{xx} =4\kappa_dh\left(1-\frac{\sin y}{y}\right)\boldsymbol P_\perp, \tag{L2} \] \[ \boldsymbol Q_{xv} =2\kappa_d\left[ (1-\cos y)\boldsymbol P_\perp +(y-\sin y)\boldsymbol J_b \right], \tag{L3} \] \[ \boldsymbol Q_{vv} =2\kappa_d\Omega^2h\,\boldsymbol P_\perp. \tag{L4} \]

Large \(|\Omega|h\) therefore does not create a three-dimensional tracer. Gyromotion rotates; it does not contract the fast velocity.

Phase-resolved response versus phase-free GC

Equations (L2)–(L4) describe the correlated, phase-resolved response. They do not by themselves describe a phase-free GC endpoint. If every child draws a fresh phase and commits its response-integrated gyration displacement, bounded gyromotion acquires a finite-step random-walk contribution. That contribution vanishes under child refinement, but it is not part of the intended same-position guiding-center closure.

12.4 Uniformly contracting tracer limit

Scale the response as

\[ \boldsymbol K_r^{(\lambda)} =\lambda\overline{\boldsymbol K}_r, \qquad \mathrm{Re}\,\lambda_i(\overline{\boldsymbol K}_r)>0, \qquad \lambda\to\infty, \tag{L5} \]

with \(\Omega/\nu\) controlled. Then the position marginal of one frozen step approaches

\[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol X_1-\boldsymbol X_0 \Rightarrow h(\boldsymbol U+\boldsymbol a_\star) +\sqrt{2h}\,\boldsymbol L_\star\boldsymbol\xi, \qquad \boldsymbol\xi\sim\mathcal N(\boldsymbol0,\boldsymbol I_3). } \tag{L6} \]

Only the slow position becomes the tracer process. The endpoint velocity remains a fast random variable. “Large gyrofrequency” by itself is not this limit.

13 · Historical inhomogeneous response

Why the old frozen phase-space drift was difficult

When a uniformly contracting response varies in space, eliminating the fast velocity produces an additional drift. This is a continuum homogenization result and must be distinguished from the coefficient-frozen step actually used by the mover.

13.1 Formal continuous homogenization

Write

\[ \boldsymbol K_r=\frac{\boldsymbol\Gamma(\boldsymbol x)}{\epsilon}, \qquad \epsilon\to0, \tag{H1} \]

and define \(\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}\) through the Lyapunov equation

\[ \boldsymbol\Gamma\boldsymbol{\mathcal J} +\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}\boldsymbol\Gamma^{\mathsf T} = 2\boldsymbol\Gamma\boldsymbol K_\star \boldsymbol\Gamma^{\mathsf T}. \tag{H2} \]

The response-gradient drift is

\[ s_{\Gamma,i} =\partial_\ell(\Gamma^{-1})_{ij}\, \mathcal J_{j\ell}. \tag{H3} \]

The limiting slow drift is \(\boldsymbol U+\boldsymbol a_{\rm micro}+\boldsymbol s_\Gamma\). To recover the selected Fickian target, the continuous microscopic model needs

\[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol a_{\rm micro} =\boldsymbol a_\star-\boldsymbol s_\Gamma. } \tag{H4} \]

As a simple check, if \(\boldsymbol\Gamma=\gamma(\boldsymbol x)\boldsymbol I\) and \(\boldsymbol K_\star=\kappa_d\boldsymbol I\), then \(\boldsymbol s_\Gamma=-\kappa_d\nabla\ln\gamma\).

13.2 What the coefficient-frozen code step does

During one accepted child, the code does not sample the spatial derivative of \(\boldsymbol\Gamma^{-1}\) along the fast stochastic motion. It holds \(\boldsymbol\Gamma,\boldsymbol K_\star\), and the drive fixed. Therefore the child generates no internal \(+\boldsymbol s_\Gamma\). Its asymptotic-preserving choice is instead

\[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol a_{\rm drive}=\boldsymbol a_\star=\nabla\kappa_d. } \tag{H5} \]
Do not put (H4) directly into the current mover

The source computes \(\boldsymbol s_\Gamma\) as a diagnostic of the formal continuous limit, but the exact frozen mean (M3) uses (H5). At finite \(\nu h\), partially resolving spatial response variation leaves a first-order truncation error; it is measured under refinement rather than canceled by adding a finite-\(h\) tensor correction.

The homogenized Fickian claim applies to an invertible, uniformly contracting, near-linear response. It does not apply to pure gyromotion or to an unfrozen state-dependent nonlinear drag rate.

The deterministic mover still uses its physical lift, drag–gyro map, all-phase certificate, and reduction. The SGS displacement is held outside that state and appended only after the GC or ORBIT endpoint is accepted. 14.1 Same-position, phase-marginal lift

For a GC state with \(J=\mu B\), draw a conditional phase only after the mode decision is known, and construct

\[ \boldsymbol x_0=\boldsymbol X_{{\rm GC},0}, \tag{T1} \] \[ \boldsymbol V_0 =\boldsymbol U+w_\parallel\bhat +\boldsymbol w_\perp(J) +\boldsymbol v_{\rm gyr}(\theta). \tag{T2} \]

14.2 Apply one physical map

The exact reference uses (M3) with \(\boldsymbol f_{\rm slow}=\boldsymbol f_{\rm GC}\) and one correlated block \((\boldsymbol\eta_x,\boldsymbol\eta_v)\). Retired V6 instead applied the two independent marginals stated in §11.2. The uniform map already damps \(|\boldsymbol v_{\rm gyr}|^2\) by \(e^{-2\bar\nu h}\). 14.3 Reduce the mapped velocity

First recover the outgoing parallel slip:

\[ w_{\parallel,1} =\bhat\cdot(\boldsymbol V_1-\boldsymbol U). \tag{T4} \]

Then use the affine slip (C8). Let \(\boldsymbol g=\boldsymbol P_\perp(\boldsymbol V_1-\boldsymbol U)-\boldsymbol w_0\) and \(\boldsymbol c_J=\partial\boldsymbol w_\perp/\partial J\). The outgoing gyration energy satisfies

\[ 2J_1=|\boldsymbol g-J_1\boldsymbol c_J|^2. \tag{T5} \]

Equivalently,

\[ F(J)=-|\boldsymbol c_J|^2J^2 +2(1+\boldsymbol g\cdot\boldsymbol c_J)J -|\boldsymbol g|^2=0. \tag{T6} \]

Define \(X=1+\boldsymbol g\cdot\boldsymbol c_J\) and \(D=X^2-|\boldsymbol c_J|^2|\boldsymbol g|^2\). The root continuous from the zero-gradient limit is

\[ \boxed{ J_1=\frac{|\boldsymbol g|^2}{X+\sqrt D}, \qquad \mu_1=\frac{J_1}{B}, } \tag{T7} \]

provided \(X>0\), \(D>0\), and the GC ordering bounds remain satisfied. The rationalized form avoids cancellation and guarantees \(J_1\ge0\). Finally,

\[ \boldsymbol X_{{\rm GC},1}=\boldsymbol x_1. \tag{T8} \]

In deployed V6, \(\boldsymbol x_1\) in (T8) includes \(\delta\boldsymbol x_\theta\). Equation (T8) is therefore the point at which a newly sampled phase contribution becomes part of the persistent GC position.

14.4 Decide the mode before drawing phase

The mapped velocity can be written as a circle in phase:

\[ \boldsymbol V_1(\theta) =\boldsymbol V_{1,0} +\boldsymbol V_c\cos\theta+\boldsymbol V_s\sin\theta, \qquad \boldsymbol V_c\cdot\boldsymbol V_s=0, \quad |\boldsymbol V_c|=|\boldsymbol V_s|. \tag{T9} \]

Before sampling \(\theta\), the algorithm bounds \(X(\theta)\), \(D(\theta)\), \(J(\theta)\), finite-Larmor-radius error, and field/flow variation over the entire circle. If any phase could fail, the child is classified as full orbit. The decision therefore cannot be biased by a favorable sampled phase.

15.1 Why the exact phase-space reference composed

The augmented homogeneous transition is

\[ \boldsymbol\Phi_h= \begin{bmatrix} \boldsymbol I&\boldsymbol M_1(h)\\ \boldsymbol0&\boldsymbol E_h \end{bmatrix}. \tag{A1} \]

For \(h=h_1+h_2\), exact Gaussian semigroup composition gives

\[ \boldsymbol\eta_{02} =\boldsymbol\Phi_{h_2}\boldsymbol\eta_{01} +\boldsymbol\eta_{12}, \qquad \boldsymbol Q_{h_1+h_2} =\boldsymbol\Phi_{h_2}\boldsymbol Q_{h_1} \boldsymbol\Phi_{h_2}^{\mathsf T} +\boldsymbol Q_{h_2}. \tag{A2} \]

This is an identity of the exact correlated frozen process. The finite-child V6 reduction set \(\boldsymbol Q_{xv}=0\), so it did not obey the semigroup identity (A2) under a changed child partition. White-direct likewise does not build a cross-partition conditional Gaussian tree; it guarantees exact reuse only of the same spatial displacement within one locked child.

15.2 Why the V6 ORBIT reduction was retired

The one-child position marginal is not the long-time diffusivity of a chain whose endpoint velocity persists. For uniform frozen coefficients with \(\nu>0\), define

\[ x=\nu h, \qquad y=\Omega h, \qquad u=x+iy, \qquad \varphi_1(z)=\frac{1-e^{-z}}{z}. \tag{A3} \]

Summing the velocity memory of the deployed \(\boldsymbol Q_{xv}=0\) ORBIT chain gives the per-component diffusion coefficients

\[ \boxed{ \frac{D_{\parallel,\rm ORBIT}}{\kappa_d} =1-\frac{(1-e^{-x})^2}{x}, } \tag{A4} \] \[ \boxed{ \frac{D_{\perp,\rm ORBIT}}{\kappa_d} =1+2\varphi_1(2x)-2\operatorname{Re}\varphi_1(u). } \tag{A5} \]

The exact correlated block supplies the missing cancellation and restores \(D_\parallel=D_\perp=\kappa_d\) for the frozen contracting process. Equations (A4)–(A5) show that the V6 reduction can under-diffuse or over-diffuse; the sign and size depend on \((x,y)\). Thus \(Q_{xv}=0\) is not a generally Fickian finite-step closure.

15.3 Why the V6 GC position circle was retired

The GC chain discards the perpendicular velocity direction but retains \(J\). For a homogeneous stationary response,

\[ \langle J\rangle_\infty =\frac{\kappa_d(\nu^2+\Omega^2)}{\nu}. \tag{A6} \]

Fresh, independent phases make successive position-circle increments uncorrelated. Combining (T2b) with the retained \(Q_{xx,\perp}\) gives

\[ \boxed{ \frac{D_{\perp,\rm GC}}{\kappa_d} =1+\varphi_1(2x)-2\operatorname{Re}\varphi_1(u) +\frac{|1-e^{-u}|^2}{2x}. } \tag{A7} \]

The final term is the fresh-phase gyration walk. At fixed \(r=\Omega/\nu\) and small \(x\),

\[ \frac{D_{\perp,\rm GC}}{\kappa_d} =\frac{1+r^2}{2}x+O(x^2). \tag{A8} \]

The closure therefore tends toward zero perpendicular transport under refinement at fixed physical rates, while at finite \(h\) it can substantially exceed \(\kappa_d\) for magnetized, weak-drag grains. The production-admissible Stage-5 ladder measured the predicted approximately \(1/M\) position-variance scaling when a root was divided into \(M\) children. That establishes the finite-child term and its refinement behavior; it does not establish \(D_{\perp,\rm GC}=\kappa_d\) at production \(h\).

Current interpretation

Equations (A4)–(A8) are frozen-coefficient diagnostics of the deployed chain, not measurements of a turbulent production run. A joint histogram of \((\nu h,|\Omega|h,\kappa_d,{\rm family},{\rm mode},{\rm tier})\) is required to determine the live magnitude. They are nevertheless sufficient to reject the claim that the present finite-child law is automatically Fickian.

15.4 A physical \(1/\Omega^2\) guiding-center diffusion is different

Hirvijoki et al. (2013) transform the particle-space Fokker–Planck collision operator into guiding-center coordinates before gyroaveraging it. Even in a uniform magnetic field, that transformation creates a real perpendicular diffusion of the guiding-center position. The origin is easiest to see directly.

During an impulsive velocity-space collision, the physical particle position \(\boldsymbol x\) is continuous. With the sign convention of (G3),

\[ \boldsymbol x=\boldsymbol X+\boldsymbol\rho, \qquad \boldsymbol\rho =\frac{\bhat\times\boldsymbol v_\perp}{\Omega}. \tag{FL1} \]

Holding \(\bhat\) and \(\Omega\) fixed over that local kick, continuity of \(\boldsymbol x\) requires

\[ \dd\boldsymbol X_{\rm coll} =-\dd\boldsymbol\rho =-\frac{\boldsymbol J_b\boldsymbol P_\perp}{\Omega} \cdot\dd\boldsymbol v_{\rm coll}, \qquad \boldsymbol J_b\boldsymbol a\equiv\bhat\times\boldsymbol a. \tag{FL2} \]

Let the local particle velocity-space diffusion tensor be

\[ \left\langle \dd\boldsymbol v_{\rm coll}\, \dd\boldsymbol v_{\rm coll}^{\mathsf T} \right\rangle =2\boldsymbol D_v\,\dd t, \qquad \boldsymbol D_v =D_\parallel\widehat{\boldsymbol v}\widehat{\boldsymbol v} +D_\perp \left(\boldsymbol I- \widehat{\boldsymbol v}\widehat{\boldsymbol v}\right). \tag{FL3} \]

Equation (FL2) pushes this covariance into position space:

\[ \boldsymbol D_{XX} =\frac{1}{\Omega^2} \left\langle \boldsymbol J_b\boldsymbol P_\perp \boldsymbol D_v \boldsymbol P_\perp\boldsymbol J_b^{\mathsf T} \right\rangle_\theta. \tag{FL4} \]

Write the pitch as \(\chi=v_\parallel/v\). Gyroaveraging the unresolved perpendicular direction gives

\[ \left\langle \boldsymbol P_\perp\widehat{\boldsymbol v} \widehat{\boldsymbol v}\boldsymbol P_\perp \right\rangle_\theta =\frac{1-\chi^2}{2}\boldsymbol P_\perp, \qquad \boldsymbol J_b\boldsymbol P_\perp \boldsymbol J_b^{\mathsf T}=\boldsymbol P_\perp. \tag{FL5} \]

Therefore

\[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol D_{XX}=\kappa_{\rm L}\boldsymbol P_\perp, \qquad \kappa_{\rm L} =\frac{ D_\perp+\tfrac12(D_\parallel-D_\perp)(1-\chi^2) }{\Omega^2} =\frac{ D_\parallel(1-\chi^2)+D_\perp(1+\chi^2) }{2\Omega^2}.} \tag{FL6} \] \[ \dd\boldsymbol X_{\rm L} =\sqrt{2\kappa_{\rm L}}\, \boldsymbol P_\perp\cdot\dd\boldsymbol W_X. \tag{FL7} \]

This is Hirvijoki et al.'s Eqs. (17) and (52), written with velocity-space rather than momentum-space diffusion coefficients; it is also Eq. (43) of the ASCOT4 method paper. The diffusion coefficient scales as \(1/\Omega^2\), while the Wiener amplitude in (FL7) scales as \(1/|\Omega|\).

Physical picture

Yes—this is Larmor-scale guiding-center jitter, with one qualification. It is not caused by an unknown gyrophase alone. A physical collision changes \(\boldsymbol v_\perp\), hence the Larmor vector \(\boldsymbol\rho\); because \(\boldsymbol x\) does not jump, \(\boldsymbol X=\boldsymbol x-\boldsymbol\rho\) must jump. If scattering changes the velocity by order \(v\) at a rate \(\nu_{\rm sc}\), then, up to pitch-angle factors, \(\kappa_{\rm L}\sim\nu_{\rm sc}(v/\Omega)^2 \sim\nu_{\rm sc}\rho_L^2\): a Larmor-radius random walk.

Why this does not rehabilitate the V6 phase walk

The Hirvijoki term is the covariance of a specified physical velocity kick. Its variance is proportional to \(\dd t\), and its long-time coefficient is independent of the numerical child partition. The V6 term instead drew an independent phase and committed \(\boldsymbol M_{1,\perp}\boldsymbol v_{\rm gyr}\); its variance was proportional to \(h^2\) per child and its effective diffusivity changed with \(h\). The two mechanisms are not the same.

Boundary for the deferred white-direct candidate

White-direct v2 contains no stochastic velocity, pitch, or magnetic-moment increment, so (FL2) has no velocity-space noise to transform. Equation (FL6) should be added only if a distinct physical \(\boldsymbol D_v\)—for example, collisional Brownian scattering or an explicitly modeled subgyro turbulent acceleration—is specified. Reusing \(\kappa_d\) to invent \(D_\parallel\) and \(D_\perp\), then adding \(\kappa_{\rm L}\) on top of the existing spatial SGS walk, would generally double-count the same unresolved transport. The apparent divergence as \(\Omega\to0\) marks failure of the GC ordering; it is not a physical infinite diffusivity and belongs in the full-orbit branch.

15.5 Deferred white-direct spatial closure

In this candidate, SGS turbulence is treated as an already homogenized grid-scale mixing model. The response factor is evaluated on \(\tau_\Delta\), the resolved deterministic GC or ORBIT state is advanced, and one spatial Itô step is then applied:

\[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol X_{n+1} =\boldsymbol X_{{\rm det},n+1} +h\boldsymbol a_{{\rm mix},{\rm code}} +\sqrt{2\kappa_dh}\,\boldsymbol\xi, \qquad \boldsymbol\xi\sim\mathcal N(\boldsymbol0,\boldsymbol I_3). } \tag{A9} \]

Here v2 uses \(\kappa_d=\kappa_0F(z,m)\) from (S10) and the code drift \(\boldsymbol a_{{\rm mix},{\rm code}}\) from (S16). This law has no stochastic velocity or moment kick, no stochastic GC phase, and no \(Q_{xv}\) approximation. It is the behavior of the deferred working-tree candidate, not of the presently selected Markov path or the older V6 binaries.

The frozen deterministic endpoint is

\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \boldsymbol V_1={}& \boldsymbol U +\boldsymbol E_h\boldsymbol W_0 +\boldsymbol M_1\boldsymbol f_{\rm mode},\\ \boldsymbol x_1={}& \boldsymbol x_0+h\boldsymbol U +\boldsymbol M_1\boldsymbol W_0 +\boldsymbol M_2\boldsymbol f_{\rm mode}. \end{aligned} } \tag{I8} \]

Only \(\boldsymbol f_{\rm mode}\) changes between GC and ORBIT. Uniform drag and Lorentz rotation are already owned by (I8). The augmented mean’s SGS drive is identically zero, so neither part of \(\boldsymbol a_{{\rm mix},{\rm code}}\) can be applied twice.

19.4 Build the child clock before sampling

Each hydro step supplies a root duration \(\Delta t_{\rm root}\). In the distributed profile, every rank walks the same tier-\(4\) clock. Its sixteen integer ticks are never accumulated in floating point:

\[ N_{\rm tick}=2^{q_{\max}}=16, \qquad h_q=\frac{\Delta t_{\rm root}}{2^q}, \qquad s_q=2^{q_{\max}-q}\ \hbox{ticks}. \tag{I11} \]

A no-draw preflight separates particles that can take the full root step from a restrictive cohort. Within the cohort, a promotion changes only the tier and retries at the same tick. If the dyadic ladder is insufficient, the overflow path can use fixed-\(2^{-52}\) root fractions; such a child fails closed if its minimum interval would violate the deterministic cap.

\[ h\leq \min(h_{\rm cell},h_{\rm phase}) \tag{I12} \]

The two terms of (I12) do not bind the same population. \(h_{\rm cell}\) is the frozen-coefficient condition: it applies to every child on either route, because it is what keeps the single midpoint gather (I2) representative. \(h_{\rm phase}\) is a requirement of the resolved ORBIT route, and the production scheduler applies it only to lanes that can take that route — those failing the guiding-center ordering. A magnetized GC child is bounded by \(h_{\rm cell}\) alone. Both are evaluated before any normal variate is generated.

Current scheduler approximation

The full mover gather evaluates \(\boldsymbol a_{{\rm mix},{\rm code}}\), but the production lean Courant gather intentionally omits \(\nabla\rho_g\), \(\nabla\nu_{\rm lin}\), and \(\nabla\Omega\). Its coarse scan evaluates only \([F-zf'w]\nabla\kappa_0\), with \(w=1-2m/3\), and adds \(\kappa_d/\Delta\) as the density-drift envelope. The v2 density rescale makes that one omitted contribution bounded, but the rate-gradient terms are still absent, and the exact per-child dust_ito_cap_check does not receive the assembled drift. The smallest correction is to fold the already-computed \(|\boldsymbol a_{{\rm mix},{\rm code}}|\) into that pre-draw cell cap and its postcheck. The lean scan can remain a cheap cohort hint.

The Gaussian displacement is only counted after the draw; it is not part of the child clock. If a cell-scale stochastic bound is desired, impose it deterministically, for example \(h\le C_X^2\Delta^2/(6\kappa_d)\) for total three-dimensional rms displacement \(\sqrt{6\kappa_dh}\le C_X\Delta\). Do not clip, reject, or resample a realized Gaussian increment.

Imposing \(h_{\rm phase}\) on every lane is the more conservative reading, and for a charged-grain population it inverts the physics. When grain charge falls with size, \(\Omega\) is largest for the smallest grains, whose gyroradii \(r_g=v_\perp/\Omega\) are correspondingly smallest; a universal phase cap therefore forces the shortest children on precisely the grains whose orbits are most tightly contained within one cell.

For scale, at the production operating point the stiffest grain reaches \(\Omega h\simeq8\) across a full root step, which is what a sixteen-tick ladder is sized to cover. A rare realized displacement larger than a cell is recorded as telemetry; it is not rejected and resampled.

Appendix A · Neutral-dust extensions

What the diffusion notes add, and what should change

Section 12 of Diffusion_notes.pdf asks how a stochastic gas displacement is transmitted to a neutral grain through drag. This is the same response question answered by the exact reference in Sections 10–12, with \(\Omega=0\). The selected white-noise closure in Section 9 uses this scalar as its drag-channel response, evaluates it over the physical SGS turnover time rather than the numerical child, and then applies the provisional magnetized trace factor \(F(z,m)\). The notes are useful because they expose the neutral factor directly and suggest a perturbative treatment of nonlinear drag. Their later finite-Courant analysis also identifies a separate, potentially useful objective: matching the numerical mass transport of a particular Eulerian scheme.

Bottom line

The constant-drag calculation supplies the parallel/neutral white-turnover factor now used inside \(\kappa_d\). It does not derive the v2 magnetized extension. The exact reference also carries velocity and position–velocity covariance, but the selected closure does not evolve those stochastic variables. The nonlinear neutral-dust derivation is worth preserving as a reference model, but it is not uniformly valid in the noise-dominated tracer limit. Flux-matched numerical diffusion could be scientifically useful as a separately named model. None of these results justifies adding another position kick or restoring the retired phase-space noise.

A.1 The neutral, constant-drag result gives the drag-channel response

Freeze \(\boldsymbol U\), \(\nu\), and \(\boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\boldsymbol\Sigma_\star^{\mathsf T} =2\boldsymbol K_\star\), and suppress the slow force and spatial drift to isolate the stochastic response. Neutral dust obeys

\[ \dd\boldsymbol X=\boldsymbol V\,\dd t, \qquad \dd\boldsymbol V =-\nu(\boldsymbol V-\boldsymbol U)\,\dd t +\nu\boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\,\dd\boldsymbol{\mathcal W}_t. \tag{ND1} \]

Integrating the same Wiener history first into velocity and then into position gives

\[ \boldsymbol\eta_v =\int_0^h\nu e^{-\nu(h-s)} \boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\,\dd\boldsymbol{\mathcal W}_s, \tag{ND2} \] \[ \boldsymbol\eta_x =\int_0^h[1-e^{-\nu(h-s)}] \boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\,\dd\boldsymbol{\mathcal W}_s. \tag{ND3} \]

With \(z=\nu h\), the scalar attenuation of the displacement variance is

\[ \boxed{ g_0^2(z) =\frac1h\int_0^h[1-e^{-\nu(h-s)}]^2\,\dd s =1-\frac{3}{2z}+\frac{2e^{-z}}{z} -\frac{e^{-2z}}{2z}.} \tag{ND4} \]

Thus \(g_0\sim z/\sqrt3\) for \(z\ll1\), while \(g_0\to1\) for \(z\gg1\). The weakly coupled grain hardly acquires the carrier displacement; the strongly coupled grain approaches the tracer increment. The compact form in (ND4) is cancellation-prone at small \(z\); its regular expansion begins \(g_0^2=z^2/3-z^3/4+O(z^4)\), as used by the current weak-response branch. The selected closure sets \(h=\tau_\Delta=\Delta^2/\kappa_0\) in this response calculation and identifies \(f(z)=g_0^2(z)\). V2 then defines \(\kappa_d=\kappa_0F(z,m)\), not simply \(\kappa_0f(z)\). Its actual integration child can be shorter or longer than \(\tau_\Delta\).

For isotropic carrier diffusivity \(\boldsymbol K_\star=\kappa_0\boldsymbol I\), the full joint covariance is

\[ q_{xx}=2\kappa_0h\,g_0^2(z), \qquad q_{xv}=\kappa_0(1-e^{-z})^2, \qquad q_{vv}=\kappa_0\nu(1-e^{-2z}). \tag{ND5} \]

Equation (ND4) is exactly the \(\Omega=0\) position scalar in (M15). The selected closure absorbs its drag-channel contribution into \(\kappa_d\), then draws one direct spatial increment during each numerical child. Equation (ND5) is retained only as the exact phase-space reference: white-direct does not draw an endpoint velocity or approximate its cross covariance.

Do not apply the response twice

The amplitude \(\sqrt{2\kappa_dh}\) already contains the response through \(\kappa_d=\kappa_0F(z,m)\). Adding a second \(g_0\)-weighted position kick would double count the response. Restoring \(\boldsymbol\eta_v\) or \(Q_{xv}\) would instead return to a different phase-space stochastic model; it is not a correction to white-direct.

A.2 A useful small-noise extension for nonlinear neutral drag

The additional idea in the notes is to retain the deterministic nonlinear drag history inside the response. Let

\[ \dot w_d=-\nu(w_d)w_d, \qquad \nu_d(t)=\nu[w_d(t)], \qquad \widehat{\boldsymbol e} =\frac{\boldsymbol w_d}{w_d}. \tag{ND6} \]

The direction \(\widehat{\boldsymbol e}\) is fixed for neutral drag alone. Linearize \(\boldsymbol w=\boldsymbol w_d+\delta\boldsymbol w\) and keep terms through first order in the noise amplitude. Define \(\nu_d'(t)=\dd\nu/\dd w|_{w_d(t)}\). With \(\boldsymbol P_w=\widehat{\boldsymbol e}\widehat{\boldsymbol e}^{\mathsf T}\) and \(\boldsymbol P_{w\perp}=\boldsymbol I-\boldsymbol P_w\),

\[ \dd\delta w_\parallel =-\left[\nu_d+w_d\nu_d'\right]\delta w_\parallel\,\dd t +\nu_d\widehat{\boldsymbol e}^{\mathsf T} \boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\,\dd\boldsymbol{\mathcal W}_t, \tag{ND7} \] \[ \dd\delta\boldsymbol w_\perp =-\nu_d\delta\boldsymbol w_\perp\,\dd t +\nu_d\boldsymbol P_{w\perp} \boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\,\dd\boldsymbol{\mathcal W}_t. \tag{ND8} \]

The resulting position kernels at the end of a child are

\[ R_{x,\parallel}(h,s) =1-\frac{w_d(h)}{w_d(s)}, \tag{ND9} \] \[ R_{x,\perp}(h,s) =\nu_d(s)\int_s^h\frac{w_d(\tau)}{w_d(s)}\,\dd\tau. \tag{ND10} \]

To replace the present joint map, however, the velocity kernels are also required:

\[ R_{v,\parallel}(h,s) =\nu_d(h)\frac{w_d(h)}{w_d(s)}, \qquad R_{v,\perp}(h,s) =\nu_d(s)\frac{w_d(h)}{w_d(s)}. \tag{ND11} \]

Define \(\boldsymbol R_a=R_{a,\parallel}\boldsymbol P_w+ R_{a,\perp}\boldsymbol P_{w\perp}\) for \(a\in\{x,v\}\). The complete tangent covariance is then

\[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol Q_{ab} =\int_0^h \boldsymbol R_a(h,s) \boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\boldsymbol\Sigma_\star^{\mathsf T} \boldsymbol R_b(h,s)^{\mathsf T}\,\dd s, \qquad a,b\in\{x,v\}.} \tag{ND12} \]

The notes’ \(g_\parallel\) and \(g_\perp\) are the normalized diagonal position integrals in (ND12). For Epstein–Baines drag, \(w_d(t)\) is the explicit solution (P5), so these integrals can be evaluated by stable one-dimensional quadrature. When \(\nu\) is constant, \(R_{x,\parallel}=R_{x,\perp}\), and (ND12) reduces to (ND2)–(ND5).

This is a tangent model, not an exact nonlinear stochastic solution

The derivation assumes \(|\delta\boldsymbol w|\ll w_d\) and drops terms of order \(\delta w\,\boldsymbol\Sigma_\star\). It obtains the leading covariance, but not the corresponding second-order correction to the mean. The slip direction also becomes undefined as \(w_d\to0\), precisely where the noise-dominated tracer limit matters most. The model is therefore suitable for an offline neutral-dust comparison, but not yet as a uniformly asymptotic-preserving production replacement.

A.3 Why the nonlinear scalar factors do not transfer directly to charged GC dust

The notes’ charged and GC subsections contain headings but no derivation. With gyromotion, the deterministic slip direction generally rotates. The linearized drag–gyro operator becomes

\[ \boldsymbol A(t) =\nu_d\boldsymbol I +w_d\nu_d'\boldsymbol P_w(t) +\Omega\boldsymbol J_b, \qquad \boldsymbol B(t) =[\nu_d\boldsymbol I+\Omega\boldsymbol J_b] \boldsymbol\Sigma_\star. \tag{ND13} \]

If \(\boldsymbol\Phi(t,s)\) solves \(\partial_t\boldsymbol\Phi=-\boldsymbol A(t)\boldsymbol\Phi\) with \(\boldsymbol\Phi(s,s)=\boldsymbol I\), the appropriate tangent block is

\[ \boldsymbol\eta_v =\int_0^h\boldsymbol\Phi(h,s)\boldsymbol B(s) \,\dd\boldsymbol{\mathcal W}_s, \tag{ND14} \] \[ \boldsymbol\eta_x =\int_0^h \left[\int_s^h\boldsymbol\Phi(t,s)\,\dd t\right] \boldsymbol B(s)\,\dd\boldsymbol{\mathcal W}_s. \tag{ND15} \]

In general, \(\boldsymbol A(t_1)\) and \(\boldsymbol A(t_2)\) do not commute. A pair of scalar attenuation factors is therefore insufficient. A charged extension would need a time-ordered three-dimensional propagator, the full joint covariance, a singular-safe factor, and the existing lift–map–reduce and fallback and restart guarantees. This is a substantial new closure and should be deferred until a neutral reference calculation shows a material error from freezing \(\nu\) over a production child.

What v2 does instead

V2 does not evaluate the charged propagator (ND13)–(ND15). It combines the neutral factor with the static magnetization fraction \(m=\Omega^2/(\nu_{\rm lin}^2+\Omega^2)\) and preserves only the scalar trace. This is a compact implemented candidate, not a consequence of the neutral-dust appendix. A time-dependent white-window derivation also depends on \(y=|\Omega|\tau_\Delta\); omitting that independent variable is the main unresolved physics question in v2.

A.4 Scheme-induced diffusion is a different target

The notes also derive diffusion from a one-jump-per-step tracer. Let a particle choose a face displacement \(\Delta\boldsymbol x_f\) with probability \(p_f\), or remain in its cell. The natural finite-step generalization of the one-dimensional calculation is

\[ \boldsymbol m=\sum_fp_f\Delta\boldsymbol x_f, \qquad \boldsymbol C =\sum_fp_f\Delta\boldsymbol x_f\Delta\boldsymbol x_f^{\mathsf T} -\boldsymbol m\boldsymbol m^{\mathsf T}, \tag{ND16} \] \[ \boxed{ \boldsymbol U_{\rm flux}=\frac{\boldsymbol m}{h}, \qquad \boldsymbol K_{\rm flux}=\frac{\boldsymbol C}{2h}.} \tag{ND17} \]

For one-dimensional, one-way advection this reduces to

\[ K_{\rm flux} =\frac12|U|\Delta x(1-|C|), \qquad C=\frac{Uh}{\Delta x}. \tag{ND18} \]

The subtraction \(-\boldsymbol m\boldsymbol m^{\mathsf T}\) is the finite-Courant correction. In several dimensions it can also produce off-diagonal covariances because choosing one face excludes every other face during that step. These terms vanish in the continuous-time limit but need not vanish at the finite hydro step.

This flux-matched process has the forward equation

\[ \partial_tn_d =-\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol U_{\rm flux}n_d) +\partial_i\partial_j(K_{{\rm flux},ij}n_d). \tag{ND19} \]

It should be distinguished from the selected concentration-Fickian target (S1). Equation (ND19) uses no additional \(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol K_{\rm flux}\) drift. Adding that drift would convert the process into Fickian diffusion and would no longer reproduce the original cell-jump law.

When a flux-matched model could be worth pursuing

It would be useful if the scientific requirement were that strongly coupled grains reproduce the finite-resolution mass transport of the Eulerian gas, rather than a prescribed physical SGS diffusivity. The implementation should then construct (ND16) from the actual multidimensional face probabilities, including AMR and the production time integrator. It should not insert the one-dimensional estimate \(D_{\rm MH}=\tfrac12a(1-C)\Delta x(1-\phi)\) directly into \(\boldsymbol K_\star\). That modified-equation coefficient is a diagnostic for a particular linear MUSCL–Hancock problem, not a general local diffusion tensor for the MHD solver.

The selected baseline instead obtains \(\kappa_d\) from the physical SGS model and uses the concentration-Fickian drift \(\nabla\kappa_d+\kappa_d\nabla\ln\rho_g\). Replacing \(\kappa_d\) by \(\boldsymbol K_{\rm flux}\), or adding the two without specifying the desired continuum equation, would mix physical turbulent transport with truncation error. A flux-matched option would also require a general tensor covariance construction; the present GPU noise layout is the isotropic, direct Cartesian three-normal walk.

A.5 The Gaussian law should remain part of the baseline

The notes correctly observe that bounded and Gaussian increments with the same first two moments share a diffusion limit after many small steps. They are not the same finite-child process. Uniform, triangular, or Bernoulli increments change higher moments and would change the restart and fallback contract. The exact correlated reference obeys the Gaussian semigroup in Section 15. White-direct preserves Gaussianity but treats a changed child partition as a different weak discretization; it makes no exact phase-space semigroup claim.

If exact cell-jump behavior is desired, it should be implemented as the mutually exclusive face process that defines (ND16), not approximated by independent Bernoulli components. For the present Gaussian–Itô model, rare tails remain accepted and are measured; deterministic preflight limits the child before sampling, and no realized draw is clipped or resampled.
