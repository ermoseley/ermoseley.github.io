Shocks

The background on the front page is incompressible — a projection method, an elliptic pressure, no shocks anywhere by construction. This is the other half of the subject: the isothermal Euler equations, solved conservatively with a Godunov scheme, driven into supersonic turbulence, running live in this tab.

What the solver actually is

Conserved state is (ρ, ρu, ρv) on a periodic, square-celled grid. Isothermal closure: P = ρcs2, with cs = 1, so every velocity printed on this page is a Mach number. That closure is not a shortcut — it is the standard approximation for the cold neutral and molecular ISM, where line cooling pins the temperature over a dynamical time. It also buys a constant sound speed, which makes the CFL condition trivial and honest, and it fits the state in three channels instead of four.

reconstruct
Piecewise linear with a minmod limiter, on the conserved variables. Minmod is the most diffusive of the standard limiters and the most robust; with a density floor and an HLL flux it is very hard to make this solver produce a negative density.
predict
MUSCL-Hancock. Both boundary-extrapolated states of a cell advance half a step using the same flux difference F(UL) − F(UR), which buys second-order accuracy in time without storing a second stage.
flux
HLL with the Davis wave-speed estimate, SL = min(uL,uR) − cs and SR = max(uL,uR) + cs. HLL has no contact wave, so it smears density contacts more than HLLC would; in exchange it is positivity-preserving for isothermal gas with no special-casing.
update
Conservative differencing of face fluxes. Mass and momentum are conserved to round-off, which is the entire point of writing it this way instead of advecting primitives.
directions
Dimensionally split, with the order Strang-alternated every step — xy, then yx — so the splitting error stays second order rather than accumulating a preferred axis. Each sweep is one GPU pass with an i−2…i+2 stencil.
timestep
Δt = C Δx / max(|u| + cs), with the maximum measured on the GPU each diagnostic interval and reduced to a handful of texels before it crosses back to the CPU. It is not a guess, and it is not fixed.

Why the histogram matters

For isothermal supersonic turbulence the density PDF is log-normal, and its width obeys σ² = ln(1 + b²ℳ²), where b depends on how the turbulence is driven: about 1/3 for purely solenoidal forcing, about 1 for purely compressive. I want to be straight about how much of that this page actually demonstrates, because the answer is less than the plot implies.

What it does demonstrate: the distribution is log-normal in shape, and it gets there on its own from a uniform initial condition. Nothing in the code knows what a log-normal is.

What it does not demonstrate: the value of b. Measured σ from a single snapshot of this box scatters by tens of per cent between settings — I ran solenoidal, mixed and compressive for sixteen seconds each and got σ = 1.87, 1.16, 1.42, which is not even monotonic in the mixture. That is not the solver being wrong; it is σ being a second moment of a heavy-tailed field sampled over about three box crossing times, which is nowhere near enough. The readout now carries a running mean for that reason, and it still should not be read to better than a factor. On top of that, σ² = ln(1 + b²ℳ²) is calibrated on three-dimensional simulations — compression in 2D makes filaments where 3D makes sheets — and the b(mixture) curve here is a straight interpolation between the two known limits rather than a measured calibration. Three sources of slop, any one of which would spoil a factor-level comparison.

The check that is airtight is the one in the readout marked mass error. A conservative scheme differences fluxes across shared faces, so what leaves one cell enters its neighbour exactly, and total mass must hold to round-off. Over twenty thousand steps it drifts by about 5 × 10⁻⁸ relative — which is float32 round-off accumulating, and nothing else. That is the claim in the update row above, tested rather than asserted.

The shocked volume readout is the fraction of the volume converging faster than a fifth of the sound speed per cell. In a supersonically driven isothermal box that fraction is small — the gas spends most of its volume in rarefied regions and most of its mass in thin shocked sheets. That asymmetry is the whole reason star formation is inefficient.

What it is not

No magnetic field, so no Alfvén waves, no magnetic pressure support, and no chance of showing the things that actually set the ISM's density structure at low Mach number. No self-gravity, so nothing collapses. No cooling function, because isothermal is the cooling function. No AMR. Two dimensions, so the vortex dynamics are wrong in the way two dimensions are always wrong. It is a demonstration of a scheme, not a science run — the real ones live in the papers.
