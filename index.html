    01 Research / particle-in-cell dust

    Dust, one grain at a time

    Interstellar dust as magnetohydrodynamic particle-in-cell superparticles: aerodynamic drag, the Lorentz force, and momentum fed back into the gas — integrated on the drift velocity so the timestep never has to resolve a Larmor orbit or a stopping time.

    [this is where you talk about the MHD-PIC dust method in RAMSES — the physical problem it was built for, why the drift-velocity formulation was the key idea, and what surprised you about the results]

    The method

    • Grains carried as massive superparticles with drag and Lorentz force, with self-consistent back-reaction on the gas.
    • Operator split on the dust–gas drift velocity: the Lorentz operator is second-order, symplectic and A-stable; the drag integrators are L-stable.
    • Three drag laws — constant stopping rate, Epstein, and Epstein with the supersonic Baines correction.
    • CIC and TSC deposition; constrained transport and an HLLD Riemann solver underneath.
    • Validated on damped gyromotion, Mach 3 and Mach 100 dusty shock tubes, and circularly polarised dusty Alfvén waves.

    02 Research / dual-frame moment method

    The dual-frame moment method

    A variational phase-space moment scheme with manifest realizability: Lagrangian geometry carried on an Eulerian grid, closed so that the numerical state can never leave the space of physically possible distributions — and so that the scheme reports exactly where its own closure is failing.

    [this is where you talk about what the dual-frame moment method is and why you and Tom Abel started building it — the problem with single-frame moment closures for cold, multi-streaming dust, and what "manifest realizability" buys you]

    Verified so far

    • Cold-limit unification. The variational action's cold limit matches the analytic Zel'dovich solution to 2.6 × 10⁻⁸, and the Hessian degeneracy localises the caustic where theory says it should be.
    • Exact passive-scalar transport. Tracer change is structurally zero — L∞ = 0.0 — against 25–114 cells of smearing for Eulerian upwind on the same problem.
    • Action-based refinement. 37.8 per cent fewer cells than gradient-based AMR at slightly better L² error, with mass, momentum and tracers conserved bit-exactly across refine/coarsen.
    • Symplectic integration in the Cholesky sector of the second-moment cone, with a realizability projection that keeps long runs alive.

    03 Research / non-ideal MHD

    When the field slips

    Molecular gas is barely ionised, so the magnetic field is only partly attached to it. Ambipolar diffusion and Ohmic dissipation on GPUs — advanced by Runge–Kutta–Gegenbauer super-time-stepping and an L-stable implicit constrained-transport solver, at the ideal-MHD timestep.

    [this is where you talk about non-ideal MHD — why ambipolar diffusion matters for star formation and for dust, and why the parabolic timestep is the thing that kills you]

    The numerics

    • Ambipolar diffusion runs at the ideal CFL step: stiffness sets the super-time-stepping stage count, never the hydro timestep. Measured stiffness ratios up to ≈ 44 in production 256³ runs.
    • An L-stable [0/2] Padé implicit constrained-transport solver, matrix-free BiCGStab with a squared shifted-diffusion preconditioner that makes the condition number stiffness-independent (≤ 1.17) — Krylov work collapses to single digits.
    • Fused RKL2 kernel: −40 % on the matched ambipolar timer on an A100, agreeing with the unfused reference to L₁ = 2 × 10⁻⁷.
    • Convergence measured on a 32³ → 256³ circularly polarised Alfvén-wave study: order 2.50 (L₁), damping rate γfit/γ → 1.000.
    • Underneath: upwind constrained transport with an HLLD Riemann solver, and EMF reflux across AMR levels.

    04 Research / cosmic rays

    Cosmic rays, self-confined

    Relativistic particles trapped by the waves they themselves grow. I follow them as superparticles inside RAMSES, and recast gyroresonant pitch-angle scattering as a stochastic differential equation so that quasi-linear transport can be integrated rather than assumed.

    [this is where you talk about cosmic ray confinement — the streaming instability, why the effective diffusion coefficient is the whole ballgame for galactic feedback, and what you are testing with particle-in-cell runs]

    What exists

    • The RAMSES MHD-PIC module built for charged dust, extended to relativistic cosmic rays with a relativistic Boris pusher and a reduced speed of light.
    • 128³ streaming-instability experiments on Stellar: CR-to-gas mass ratio 10⁻⁹, c̃/c = 6 × 10⁻⁴, plasma β = 1, γ₀ = 10 — with measured growth of density, magnetic and velocity fluctuations.
    • Pitch-angle scattering written as an SDE with quasi-linear Fokker–Planck coefficients, so scattering is integrated alongside the orbit instead of being imposed.
    • Co-author on Cosmic ray and plasma coupling for isothermal supersonic turbulence in the magnetised interstellar medium (Sampson, Beattie, Teyssier, Kempski, Moseley, Commerçon, Dubois & Rosdahl), arXiv:2506.03768 — a two-moment CRMHD study of coupled and decoupled regimes.

    05 Code / phrike

    phrike

    φρίκη — a shudder. A Fourier pseudo-spectral solver for compressible Euler and non-ideal MHD that runs identically on NumPy and on a GPU, and that advances the stiff diffusion with exponential-in-time operators instead of sub-stepping through it.

    There is a small relative of it running in your browser: Shocks — the isothermal Euler equations solved conservatively with a Godunov scheme, driven into supersonic turbulence, with the density PDF measured live. Not spectral, and not phrike, but the same instinct.

    [this is where you talk about why you wrote phrike — what you wanted that existing codes would not give you, and what it is for now]

    Design

    • Fourier collocation with pointwise physical-space fluxes, 2/3-rule dealiasing and a high-order exponential modal filter; a Legendre–Gauss–Lobatto basis is available in 1D for non-periodic boundaries.
    • Constant Ohmic diffusion is exact in Fourier space. Variable-η Ohmic and ambipolar diffusion use frozen-coefficient matrix-exponential action, or a second-order L-stable rational resolvent solved matrix-free by preconditioned conjugate gradients.
    • Embedded adaptive Runge–Kutta (RK23 / RK45 / RK78) with PI step-size control, or plain RK2/RK4 under a wave-speed CFL.
    • Solenoidal projection of B every step, so ∇·B stays at roundoff.
    • One duck-typed array backend: NumPy/SciPy FFT, pyFFTW, or PyTorch on CUDA, Apple MPS or CPU — same numerics, single or double precision.
    • Tests assert physics, not fixtures: exact Ohmic damping to roundoff, dt³ one-step error for the resolvent, and dust relaxing to its fluctuation–dissipation equilibrium.

    Scope, stated plainly

    Adaptive in time only — there is no mesh refinement and no MPI. Euler runs in 1D, 2D and 3D; MHD and the dust moment fluid are 1D and 2D with three-component vectors.

    04 Play / the gas behind the page

    Turn the knobs

    Every switch here belongs to the solver running behind the type, and there is no second copy of the state for it to disagree with: the engine publishes the list, this chapter renders whatever is in it, and each change lands in the box you are already looking at.

    Some of these are display and most of them are physics, and a few will happily spoil the picture — which is the reason to have them. Nothing is made true by being adjustable: β = 40 driven at Mach 2.5 is a perfectly legitimate thing to ask this scheme for and a poor thing to believe.

    Controls arrive with the simulation.

    Kept for this tab. What you change is written to sessionStorage and applied again the next time this chapter loads. It is deliberately not in the URL: the router reads a hash it does not recognise as the Cover, so a settings string written there would throw you back to the front page mid-drag. Close the tab and the solver is back to its own defaults.

    readoutwaiting for the solver

    The numbers. The closure is isothermal with cs = 1, so a velocity and a Mach number are the same thing here, and β fixes the Alfvén speed at √(2/β). mass is the mean density: the update is conservative, so it should sit on its starting value to roundoff, and a number that drifts is a bug rather than physics. ∇·B is the rms and the worst cell, in units of |∇·B| Δx / |B|. Constrained transport keeps it at floating-point roundoff; the selectable Dedner path cleans it toward zero. The reconstruction line is what ran on the last step rather than what is selected: PLM takes the box back for a second and a half after anything disturbs it.

    What's going on in the background?

    Colophon. The background is compressible and magnetised: the isothermal MHD equations solved conservatively on a periodic — grid, with a vertical mean field, at plasma β = 5 — so the seed field is sqrt(2/5) = 0.63, the Alfvén speed is 0.63 against a sound speed of 1, and the flow at rms Mach 0.7 is mildly super-Alfvénic. That is the regime where the turbulence wins: the field gets tangled and amplified by the flow rather than organising it. Every number in this paragraph is a control — see Play, the last chapter, which exposes the solver, the field, the grains and the palette and lets you take the magnetic field out entirely.   ∇·B is controlled by constrained transport, the reference configuration: Bx and By live on cell faces, a half-step predictor time-centres them, and mini-RAMSES's two-dimensional HLLD solver returns one EMF at each corner. The same number is differenced into both faces it borders with opposite signs, so the discrete divergence cancels to floating-point roundoff instead of being repaired afterwards. Dedner cleaning remains selectable in Play: there B is cell-centred and ψ carries divergence error at the fastest signal speed while being damped, with the Powell source removing its spurious field-aligned force.   The flux is HLLD — Miyoshi & Kusano, the reference's own hlld_mhd_fluxes reduced to isothermal and to two dimensions. Five waves: two fast, two Alfvén, and the contact, so the transverse field and velocity are rotated through the Alfvén waves instead of being averaged across them, which is the whole reason to pay for it. Reconstruction is piecewise linear — slope_type = 2, the MonCen limiter from uslope, on all seven primitives, with a half-step predictor in the primitive variables.   There is also a selectable piecewise parabolic reconstruction: Colella & Woodward on all seven primitives. It is retained with the Dedner path; selecting it switches from CT because the reference CT trace above is PLM. When selected, it runs while the box is quiet. Its one MHD-specific subtlety is worth stating, because getting it wrong would quietly undo the cleaning. The PPM face state is the parabola averaged over the slab the flow crosses in half a step, which carries the −u ∂q/∂x term for you at one speed for every variable. That is fine for the hydrodynamic variables, all of which are advected along the normal. It is simply wrong for two of the three field variables: Bn has no such term at all — the normal component is advected only transversely, which is the fact constrained transport is built around — and ψ is not advected by anything. So the field takes the slab average in its transverse component and the plain parabola edge in the other two. PLM is what runs for a second and a half after anything disturbs the box, either way: a parabola fitted across a freshly injected discontinuity is monotonised variable by variable, and a state monotone in every variable separately can still be unphysical taken together — which is fragile enough in hydrodynamics without two of the variables being a field whose divergence is meant to vanish.   What is not drawn by default is the shock fronts. The unmagnetised page at hydro paints the convergence of the velocity field, because a captured discontinuity is the thing an incompressible background cannot show at all; here the fronts are long-lived, and with the field itself drawn they turned out to be a second overlay competing for the same picture. S puts them back — nothing numerical changes either way, and the pass that prepares them does not even run while they are off.   The field itself is drawn, and you can turn it off with B. It is a line integral convolution: walk a few cells along the field line through both ends of every pixel, average a static noise field over the walk, and what comes back is a texture whose grain lies along B. That is the right choice here for a reason that is not only aesthetic — the grains at charge 100 are already stuck to field lines, so the streaks run the way the dust does, and the field reinforces what the dust is telling you instead of annotating it. It is signed about zero, so it grains the medium rather than lighting it and the mean brightness of the frame does not move; it is drawn in the chapter's own accent; and it is scaled by |B|/B0, so a stretched field shows itself and an undisturbed one stays quiet. The kernel travels along the walk, which sends bright packets running along the field lines — the one motion in the picture that is not advective, which is what an Alfvén wave is.   The dust is charged, charge-to-mass 100 for the whole population, and the update is mini-ramses's. The force acts on the drift w = v − ugas, which is not a shortcut: in ideal MHD the lab-frame electric field is −u×B, so the whole Lorentz force is q w×B and a grain moving with the gas feels nothing. The rotation is the reference's implicit Cayley form, (I − ½δM)−1(I + ½δM), which is exact for any step: it preserves |w| to roundoff and cannot heat the population. Then drag, as one backward Euler stage after it rather than the reference's half-drag / Lorentz / half-drag split.   The grains are two-and-a-half dimensional and the gas is not. B lies in the plane, so w×B for an in-plane drift points out of it and a charged grain cannot stay planar even in a planar flow; the population carries three velocity components. The gas needs none, because with Bz = vz = 0 their equations are homogeneous and they stay zero exactly. At charge 100 the gyroradius is a twelfth of a cell, so the grains are stuck to field lines: — grains, stopping times drawn log-uniformly over 1.4 decades, and what they draw here is the field rather than the flow.   One consequence worth stating, because it looks like a regression and is not. A scroll deposits a shear layer across mid-screen, and the mean field threads it at right angles. Field-line tension resists the roll-up — a layer is stable to Kelvin–Helmholtz once Δu < 2vA. At β = 1 that was 2.8 sound speeds against the 1.4 a gesture deposits, and a scroll made Alfvén waves and current sheets where the unmagnetised page makes vortices. At β = 5 it is 1.26 against the same 1.4, so the layer is unstable again and the rolls come back — turn β down in Play and watch them stop.   The two schemes this one grew out of are both still here, running the same deck: the compressible hydrodynamics at hydro — same solver family, no field, neutral grains — and the incompressible original at projection, which advects semi-Lagrangian and solves an elliptic pressure by multigrid, and has no shocks in it anywhere by construction. Written from scratch in WebGL2, no libraries.
